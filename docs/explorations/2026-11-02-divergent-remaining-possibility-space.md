## Change the question before generating options

Earlier exploration primarily imagined richer **reasoning, recollection, reinterpretation, knowledge interfaces and developer experiences**. In this pass the main shift is to ask about different *ends*, different *affected beings*, and different *substrates*:

| Inherited default (a lens, not an asserted repository defect) | Counter-question |
| --- | --- |
| Better knowledge helps build or reason about software | What if knowledge helps people **need less software**? |
| Capturing more source-grounded knowledge is helpful | What if a person should be able to **decline being modeled at all**? |
| Consequences live in code, product and engineering work | What if the consequence is **electricity, water, repair labor, a local place or an ecosystem**? |
| Architecture is understood through text, diagrams and queries | What could a body learn through **movement, timing or material practice**? |
| The relevant beneficiary is the repository's user, owner or developer | What about someone **affected but never enrolled**, or a future custodian after the software vanishes? |
| Authority means who may decide, execute or publish | How could the rationale itself become **publicly contestable** without granting those people repository execution authority? |

These are temporary imaginative inversions, **not** claims that the accepted product strategy is wrong or must broaden.

## Family Q - Successful exit and the right to remain outside knowledge

**New goal class:** users' ability to thrive when less software, or less knowledge capture, is the desired end. **Source seed:** ADR 0034 makes construction optional; source-boundary and semantic-authority invariants restrict what stored knowledge can mean. Existing candidates focus on persistent memory, selective foregrounding or consent to share already-created information.

### RP-Q1 - The software that succeeds by disappearing

**Possible experience:** a community service asks a repository-grounded agent to imagine what a *successful exit* from its own software would look like. Instead of a new interface, it receives a gallery of possible futures in which people preserve the underlying benefit through a human practice, portable record, simple local tool, or no ongoing product at all. The product goal is **freedom from continued software dependency**, not another technical migration.

**Difference:** [NX-P1 ritual archaeology](2026-11-02-divergent-repository-knowledge-second-pass.md#nx-p1---ritual-archaeology) discovers social functions inside existing routines; this asks whether *the software itself* can cease to be necessary. [NX-L1 promise-preservation](2026-11-02-divergent-repository-knowledge-second-pass.md#nx-l1---promise-preservation-studio) changes product form while conserving a promise; this explicitly allows the *product to stop existing*. Also belongs to **T** (inheritance after disappearance).

### RP-Q2 - A right not to become a semantic object

**Possible experience:** someone whose behavior could be inferred from repository logs or documents asks, “What might it mean for me to remain unrepresented?” A counterfactual design studio imagines workflows in which some questions are intentionally not answered because gathering their evidence would defeat an important human purpose. The desired outcome is **non-capture and non-inference as a positive form of agency**, not an incomplete database.

**Difference:** [NX-O1 knowledge escrow](2026-11-02-divergent-repository-knowledge-second-pass.md#nx-o1---knowledge-escrow-for-imagination) conditionally shares *existing* knowledge; [NX-M1 reconstructible oblivion](2026-11-02-divergent-repository-knowledge-second-pass.md#nx-m1---reconstructible-oblivion) hides recoverable knowledge. Here the information **does not become a claim in the first place**. The source-boundary invariant inspires the distinction but does not itself create a person's right to erase, prohibit or alter actual repository records. Also belongs to **T** and **U**.

## Family R - Material and ecological entanglement

**New goal class:** understand software as one participant in energy, hardware, repair, environmental and place-based systems. **Source seed:** the ontology describes software components, dependencies and data flows; the change-impact Skill tracks bounded consequences and external dependencies. The second divergent pass explicitly left physical-environment and ecological dimensions unexamined.

### RP-R1 - Material biographies of a product decision

**Possible experience:** a developer wonders what *physical life* a proposed user experience might have: devices manufactured and maintained, networks relied upon, electricity consumed, cooling and water used, hardware eventually replaced. The creative artifact is a set of **hypothetical material biographies** showing radically different ways the same user outcome could inhabit the physical world. No footprint number or real-world impact is inferred from source-code structure alone.

**Difference:** [DE-J1 generative architectural affordances](2026-11-02-divergent-repository-knowledge.md#family-j---generative-architecture) imagines experiences an architecture permits; this imagines the **physical metabolism of the experience beyond the repository**. Existing change-impact analysis follows engineering surfaces; it does not establish resource measurements. Also belongs to **U** (who bears material costs).

### RP-R2 - Place-bound product imagination

**Possible experience:** replay an otherwise coherent software promise in a battery-powered rural workshop, a public library with interrupted connectivity, and a shared physical terminal. Instead of only adapting an interface, the inquiry may invent **different goals**: local autonomy, repairability, shared stewardship, or offline practices whose value cannot be reduced to a single cloud feature.

**Difference:** [NX-N1 applicability envelopes](2026-11-02-divergent-repository-knowledge-second-pass.md#nx-n1---applicability-envelopes) asks where an *engineering claim* applies. This asks how the **material place changes what people should want from a product**, whether or not the code is valid. The imagined places are illustrations, not inspected target repositories or demographic evidence. Also belongs to **S** and **T**.

### RP-R3 - More-than-human beneficiaries

**Possible experience:** ask whether a software design might be imagined as serving a watershed, habitat or equipment ecosystem—not because those beings “use” an app or make authorized decisions, but because human observers might reason about their affected interests. A set of conditional vignettes could distinguish direct users, environmental proxies, and claims that remain scientifically unknown.

**Difference:** [DE-B1 role migration](2026-11-02-divergent-repository-knowledge.md#family-b---role-migration) changes a capability's intended human beneficiary; this considers **non-human affected systems that cannot participate in the normal user role**. It also differs from RP-R1, whose object is the material biography of a design, not the plurality of beings it could affect. No ecosystem facts or consent are asserted. Also belongs to **T** and **U**.

## Family S - Embodied and temporal ways of knowing

**New goal class:** knowing architecture through physical participation and experienced rhythm, not only retrieving propositions or contemplating a fiction. **Source seed:** the ontology distinguishes contracts, data flow and control flow; the stewardship pack preserves incidents and lessons; earlier explorations used maps, cases and stories without treating lived movement/time as the primary medium.

### RP-S1 - Architecture as a bodily rehearsal

**Possible experience:** a small group enacts a repository's documented roles and boundaries with movements, objects and spatial arrangements. The participants pass a symbolic artifact between positions, discover where responsibility becomes ambiguous, and imagine alternative social arrangements *as felt interactions*. The aim is **embodied comprehension and collaborative invention**, not an automated dependency diagram or a runtime test.

**Difference:** [DE-F1 explorable repository](2026-11-02-divergent-repository-knowledge.md#family-f---exploring-repositories) organizes navigation through meanings; [DE-F2 design-fiction workshop](2026-11-02-divergent-repository-knowledge.md#family-f---exploring-repositories) narrates hypothetical use. Here **the participants' physical coordination is the medium that generates questions**. It is not verification of actual software behavior. Also belongs to **U** when enacted roles include authority or social power.

### RP-S2 - Temporal atmospheres of software

**Possible experience:** imagine the *tempo* of a software service as lived by a caregiver, student, operator or interrupted worker: waiting, sudden demands, repetition, bursts of urgency and quiet intervals. Repository knowledge about workflows could inspire different **temporal habitats**—for example, systems that respect unbroken attention or collective pacing—instead of merely increasing throughput.

**Difference:** [DE-H1 experiential concept discovery](2026-11-02-divergent-repository-knowledge.md#family-h---outside-the-ontology) names emotional outcomes generally; this makes **duration, bodily interruption and rhythm** the unit of imaginative design. [NX-K3 parallel observation worlds](2026-11-02-divergent-repository-knowledge-second-pass.md#nx-k3---parallel-observation-worlds) changes what evidence is observed, whereas this changes what kind of *lived time* a future product could produce. Nothing in source code alone proves the described feelings. Also belongs to **R** (place) and **T** (people living with its consequences).

## Family T - Unenrolled publics and post-repository inheritance

**New goal class:** create intelligibility and agency for those who did not choose to become product users, or who inherit consequences after today's developers and repositories are gone. **Source seed:** the product strategy explicitly names the present high-delegation owner as primary user; history, provenance and exact-revision knowledge are source-grounded; authority remains bounded to current legitimate actors.

### RP-T1 - The experience of people who never signed up

**Possible experience:** a public-service repository's future is explored from the standpoint of a neighbor, visitor, worker or household affected by outputs without ever receiving an account. Their possible goals are neither convenience features nor developer productivity: **understanding, challenge, distance, repair of downstream effects, or not having to participate**.

**Difference:** [DE-B1 role migration](2026-11-02-divergent-repository-knowledge.md#family-b---role-migration) transfers a capability to another role or user; [NX-P2 participation horizons](2026-11-02-divergent-repository-knowledge-second-pass.md#nx-p2---participation-horizons) asks who can contribute later. These people may **never use or contribute to the system at all**, yet remain affected. This is a counterfactual product-public expansion, not evidence such publics currently have any right to modify Sensemaking. Also belongs to **Q** and **U**.

### RP-T2 - Inheritance after the repository disappears

**Possible experience:** a future community custodian, museum archivist or service recipient confronts remnants of a system whose developers, issue tracker and product company no longer exist. Instead of teaching engineering successors, an imagined knowledge experience helps them **understand what past software did to their world and what remains safe to remember, contest or leave behind**.

**Difference:** [DE-E3 design-judgment inheritance](2026-11-02-divergent-repository-knowledge.md#family-e---architectural-apprenticeship) transfers engineering judgment to later agents; [NX-L3 multi-era meaning lenses](2026-11-02-divergent-repository-knowledge-second-pass.md#nx-l3---multi-era-meaning-lenses) interprets historical terms. Here the beneficiary is **outside the engineering lineage, after the product's institutional continuity ends**. It does not imply indefinite hosting, guaranteed reconstruction, legal preservation or altered retention rules. Also belongs to **Q** and **U**.

## Family U - Civic legitimacy and distribution of consequences

**New goal class:** explore public contestability and unequal burdens as product outcomes, distinct from repo-owner authority to decide or publish. **Source seed:** ADR 0029 separates source-grounded knowledge, judgments and action authority; the product ontology distinguishes goals, affected capabilities and evidence; change-impact guidance asks about material consequences without proving them.

### RP-U1 - Public contest without repository control

**Possible experience:** an affected community can examine several source-grounded narratives about a proposed service's purpose, ask “whose assumptions were treated as facts?”, and author alternative questions or objections. The imaginative outcome is **legible, contestable justification**, not automatic votes, merge rights, product-thesis ratification or a new source of truth.

**Difference:** [DE-G1 design disagreement laboratory](2026-11-02-divergent-repository-knowledge.md#family-g---creative-disagreement) studies competing interpretations of a problem; [NX-O3 delegation-shaped possibility rooms](2026-11-02-divergent-repository-knowledge-second-pass.md#nx-o3---delegation-shaped-possibility-rooms) separates permission to explore, recommend and decide. This asks whether **people outside those roles have standing to question the *legitimacy* of the goal itself**, irrespective of whether they can perform any repository operation. Also belongs to **T** and **Q**.

### RP-U2 - Who benefits, who absorbs the burden?

**Possible experience:** a speculative “consequence commons” explores how one change may deliver convenience to one group while making repair work, waiting time, ecological burdens or dependence fall on others. The product goal is **visibility of unequal allocation**, including downstream people and nonhuman interests, rather than a single total-benefit story. Claims of impacts remain clearly hypothetical unless independent evidence is supplied.

**Difference:** [NX-P3 institutional feedback worlds](2026-11-02-divergent-repository-knowledge-second-pass.md#nx-p3---institutional-feedback-worlds) imagines software and human practices reshaping each other; RP-U2 asks **how benefits and burdens are distributed, including to nonparticipants**. RP-R1 traces material conditions without making their distribution the main creative object. Also belongs to **R** and **T**.

## Relationships, variations and unrepresented dimensions

The families describe *overlapping conceptual territories*, not mutually exclusive classifications. These are exploration-local relationships, **not** typed `rel:` edges, architectural dependencies, causal predictions or recommendations.

| Cross-family intersection | Additional, still-unexplored possibility it opens |
| --- | --- |
| **RP-Q1 + RP-T2** (Q / T) | Could a software system intentionally support a good life *after the software and its institutions are gone*? |
| **RP-Q2 + RP-U1** (Q / U) | Could a person legitimately challenge a system without first becoming its subject of knowledge capture? |
| **RP-R1 + RP-U2** (R / U) | How could material dependencies and the unequal burden of keeping them running be imagined together without fabricating measurements? |
| **RP-R2 + RP-S2** (R / S) | Could electricity, place and social rhythms generate genuinely different product goals from the same software code? |
| **RP-R3 + RP-T1** (R / T) | Which affected beings or households are missing when “user” means “authenticated participant”? |
| **RP-S1 + RP-U1** (S / U) | Could physically rehearsing authority boundaries let people articulate legitimacy concerns that diagrams alone leave tacit? |
| **RP-T2 + RP-U2** (T / U) | How might burdens and benefits outlive the original repository and its ability to answer questions? |

### Open variables, not a new ontology

- **Existence of software:** always-on service; replaceable local practice; voluntary retirement; absent product.
- **Right to knowledge:** attributable public evidence; consenting first-person testimony; undisclosed information; intentionally uncollected information.
- **Physical substrate:** cloud dependency; local device; shared terminal; non-digital human coordination; ecological surroundings.
- **Medium of understanding:** proposition; movement; perceived tempo; repaired physical artifact; communal hearing.
- **Affected party:** authenticated user; indirect neighbor or worker; future custodian; nonhuman system represented only by bounded human observation.
- **Object of justification:** correct implementation; coherent meaning; legitimate purpose; distribution of benefits/burdens.

These variables are questions for future imagination, not scales, schema fields or a completeness checklist.

### Further territory still outside this pass

This pass has **not** substantively explored machine-to-machine epistemic negotiation, cultural differences in legitimate knowledge practices, disability- and care-specific relational experiences, contested ownership of inherited software artifacts, non-textual long-term preservation, or how software reshapes labor outside formal engineering organizations. These are *remaining imaginative gaps*, not planned work or promises of an inexhaustible idea supply.

## Qualitative reflection on divergence and diminishing returns

**Coverage observation:** this pass reached materially different conceptual dimensions: *successful disappearance instead of product growth, the choice not to be modeled instead of richer memory, software's physical/ecological relationships instead of code-only consequences, embodied temporal understanding instead of text-first comprehension, affected non-users and distant inheritors instead of engineering audiences, and civic legitimacy/distribution instead of internal authority alone*. These are new **kinds of desired outcomes and affected worlds** relative to the bounded first/second-pass comparison, not merely fresh interface names.

**Where novelty became harder:** several tempting extensions were recognizable rephrasings of earlier memory, stakeholder, fiction and applicability candidates; the explicit restatement list above preserves that distinction. Some new families also meet existing ones at their edges (e.g. place and situated validity, public contest and permission-shaped exploration). Those overlaps are recorded rather than used to claim total originality.

**Coverage remains open:** no completeness proof, global novelty claim, performance observation or quality judgment follows. Qualitative diminishing returns are visible when repeatedly asking the earlier questions, but changing the *goal*, *medium* and *affected party* opened genuinely different territory. Whether another pass yields further conceptual change cannot be inferred from the number of records.

**Retention boundary:** the curated [Idea Library](../idea-library.md), `.sensemaking/knowledge/`, strategy, ADRs, Skills, schemas, tests, `STATUS.md` and architectural commitments are unchanged. No `RP-` idea becomes a `poss:`, `arch:`, `conseq:`, task or authorized action. The end state is this dated exploratory record and its atlas navigation only.
