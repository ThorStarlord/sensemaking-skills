## 2. Two directions at equivalent comparison level

**B — Longitudinal Engineering Judgment (original rank 3).** Distinct intended outcome: later coding agents and owners can **recover, reconsider and transmit the reasoning behind decisions** so that changed context does not turn old knowledge into an unexamined veto or a source of repeated rediscovery. These records are not independent evidence of successful longitudinal judgment.

**A — Architectural Possibility Generation (original rank 5).** Distinct potential outcome: an agent's source-grounded understanding of **capabilities, architectural alternatives, rejected mechanisms, contracts and conditional effects** can make **different classes of user experience/product future imaginable**, rather than merely producing a second implementation for a nominated goal. Original identities include **GD-A/B/G, DE-C1/C2/C3, DE-D1, DE-H2, DE-I1/I2/I3, DE-J1/J2/J3** and adjacent **DE-G2**. Curated **poss:cross-product-transfer** and **poss:assumption-aware-opportunity-ecology** are close comparators, not new products discovered here. Architectural exaptation, conceptual fission, capability chemistry and optionality are **generative mechanisms**, not user-valued outcomes by themselves.

**Comparability condition:** both are evaluated for *next conceptual understanding of a distinctive product outcome*, not for which technology is easier to code. Longitudinal history is a strongly accepted product destination; architectural generativity is a promising but unproven effect of retained source relationships. Accepting the former does not make a follow-up inquiry automatically more valuable, nor does novelty of the latter establish product merit.

These are **conceptual source relationships**, not a demonstrated automatic generator, and do not make `mayEnable` into proof of feasibility or user demand.
- **Original exploratory creative seeds:** **GD-B** (exaptation of rejected architecture fragments), **GD-G** (optionality), **DE-I2** (product-pattern fission), **DE-H2** (conceptual metamorphosis), **DE-C2** (contract inversion), **DE-J1/J3** (generative affordances and future optionality). - **Existing related experiments in reasoning, *not empirical benefit evidence*:** [Generative Depth, mechanism F](2026-11-02-generative-depth-repository-knowledge.md) already suggests that the aspiration for orientation/continuity behind a rejected automatic planner might inspire owner-directed experiences. It is an important counterexample: structural alternatives and good architecture decisions do **not** imply new user ends.

**One selected conceptual premise (not an architecture proposal):** suppose a capable coding agent could **split apart** the *organizing/comparative affordance* motivating a rejected automatic selection mechanism from its **rejected decision authority**, and creatively reinterpret that affordance through already documented alternative, constraint and conditional-consequence relationships. Rather than choose a “next task,” it considers what qualitatively different owner/user **experiences and desired ends** become conceivable if architectural relationships are treated as material for inquiry.

This is not a design for a planner, UI, ranking engine, simulation, graph traversal or automatic output pipeline. Its **specific source relationship** is *rejected automatic selection* ↔ *accepted attributed architecture/consequence/possible-goal reasoning with owner authority*. The **beneficiary** is the high-delegation repository owner aided by an active coding agent; a different product/user audience would require an explicit hypothesis and potentially thesis review. The original rejected mechanism remains rejected for present action.

## 5. Decision, non-comparable frontiers and reassessment conditions

**Dated pairwise result:** **A — Architectural Possibility Generation** provisionally **precedes B — Longitudinal Engineering Judgment as the next focused conceptual inquiry among these two**. This **locally revises the comparative preference** implicit in the original rank 3 versus rank 5, while **leaving #715's historical ordered portfolio unchanged**. It does **not** re-rank the other six outcome directions, declare architectural generation a better product, prefer an implementation, or automatically launch the proposed Depth Exploration.

**Intellectual diversity check:** prior work has emphasized contextual truth, authority, historical soundness and plural interpretation. The new A preference is **not** a compensatory bonus for “creativity.” Its warrant is the demonstrably unexamined *consequence object*—whether reinterpreting rejected architectural relationships can open **qualitatively different desired user experiences**—and the bounded real rejected architecture/accepted authority relationship. This is still a hypothesis about expected learning, not about proven human value.

**Transformative frontier retained:** [Non-capture/voluntary exit](2026-11-03-depth-exploration-prioritization.md#3-transformative-directions-parallel-non-comparable-frontier) and material/ecological/public consequences remain separate conditional directions with different beneficiary/evidence/Level-4 premises. Using the word “user” in this inquiry does not silently authorize a broader civic or nonhuman beneficiary, and two architectural options do not establish a product-thesis change. Other unexamined directions remain available under the original portfolio and ordinary separate authority.

**Reconsider this local preference when:**

1. **A collapses into prior records.** A future source-grounded exploration finds the apparent novel owner experience is simply `poss:assumption-aware-opportunity-ecology`, `poss:possibility-memory`, a nicer Idea Library view or the already documented architecture-synthesis process. The main remaining value of more A depth then falls.
2. **B gains a materially distinct consequence.** A genuine later independent-agent episode shows that *preserving a change of question* across sessions changes a source-qualified decision or systematically harms future inquiry in a way #717–#719 cannot explain. B could regain priority.
3. **An architectural relationship proves mistaken or already exhausted.** New source inspection shows the hypothesized planner–ontology relationship is not meaningful beyond familiar mechanisms, or a different owner product need cannot be grounded. Stop or choose a different warranted target; no automatic new divergence.
4. **Governing intent changes.** An owner-ratified strategy/ADR materially changes who the beneficiaries are, what authority is delegated or which outcomes define success. Reassess under the new authority, preserving the dated historical order.
5. **A new distinct possibility or beneficiary emerges.** If a consequence falls outside the existing eight directions, consider separately warranted divergence/coverage or thesis-aware inquiry; do not force it into this pairwise preference.
6. **Different question needs empirical evidence.** A source-only agent may invent the same new experience; no graph-caused or longitudinal benefit follows from a thought experiment. When the next decision depends on comparative efficacy, the evidence inquiry remains a *separate, conditional responsibility*.

**Non-triggers:** document volume, candidate count, an attractive metaphor, a PR merge, a green validator, increased graph connectivity, an extra hypothetical architecture option or a newly coined product name. These do not by themselves alter intellectual opportunity or source-grounded value.
