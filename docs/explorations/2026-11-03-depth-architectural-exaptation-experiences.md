## 2. One transformation: from selecting alternatives to authoring the question

**Original normative/architectural tension:** a system might relieve a repository owner's burden by *ranking* alternative responsibilities and *selecting* the next one, but doing that mechanically would take semantic/strategic authority the current product explicitly reserves to the active agent and owner processes. The valid underlying concern—making complex architectural alternatives and their consequences intellectually usable—survives the rejected means.

**Creative operation:** **separate the purpose of comparing possibilities from the authority to make a selection; then change the object of the comparison from “which task should win?” to “which beneficiary experience is our current task set failing to ask about?”**

This has three distinct moves, each conditional:

1. **Fission:** retain source- and authority-bounded *relationships among alternatives, constraints and possible consequences*; discard automatic task ranking and selection. This division is supported by the coexistence of ADR 0029's rejection and the ontology's prospective relations, **not** evidence of a specific new implementation.
2. **Inversion:** do not assume the accepted list of goals is the complete universe. Treat the reasons an approach was declined as potential **questions about the human experiences the governing boundary protects or excludes**. The direction of reasoning changes from `named goal → implementation candidates` toward `historical architectural relation → conditional beneficiary experience → question whether a different goal is wanted`.
3. **Beneficiary test:** investigate whether an owner or other legitimate participant could **recognize a new desire or missing kind of participation** from the conditional experience, compared with merely seeing a new presentation of the same alternatives. The desire is **not in the source record** and may not arise.

**Conditional causal chain (not a proven graph traversal):**

`Historical rejected ranking/selection + accepted source/authority boundaries`  
→ **logical distinction:** comparing reasons is not itself automatic selection  
→ **hypothetical agent move:** translate the comparison/rejection into a first-person question about who may originate product questions  
→ **hypothetical human response:** the owner might value a different sort of participant agency, rather than faster selection  
→ **creative possibility:** a source-qualified, non-authoritative experience for *formulating worthwhile product questions before choosing construction*.

**Breaks:** the agent may merely restate DE-A3/DE-B2; a competent source-only agent may do exactly this; the owner may not want the proposed experience; the imagined beneficiary may not exist or value it; source-linked architecture cannot establish subjective needs; no link in the current knowledge graph generates this experience automatically.

**What changes conceptually:** the *criterion for a good outcome* could migrate from “correct, efficient selection of work already in view” to “a legitimate person can question what the software ought to make possible at all.” That outcome is not required by ADR 0029 and is not automatically a new user goal. It is the **consequence object** this Depth Exploration tests.

## 3. One concrete hypothetical beneficiary experience

### Fictional encounter: from “choose my next task” to “who is allowed to pose a product question?”

**Status: HYPOTHETICAL DESIGN FICTION.** No owner, contributor or coding agent is reported to have experienced this. The conversation below is illustrative, not a transcript, product proposal or confirmed requirement.

**Initial situation:** imagine the repository owner wants to make choosing a development direction easier. The agent has retained the rejected ranked-backlog/planner rationale and present-source records of architecture alternatives. A *hypothetical newcomer contributor* can read source documents and has noticed that major decisions seem predetermined, but nobody has established whether such a newcomer actually feels excluded.

**Existing documented limitation:** Sensemaking can preserve, retrieve and reason about goals, decisions, alternatives, source links and authority. Its accepted product description does not establish a working, independently validated user experience in which a newcomer originates a previously unasked **product purpose** and the owner can distinguish that proposal from a construction request. The *idea* of participatory creation is already in **DE-B2**; the observed capability is **NOT_ASSESSED**, not absent because no graph node names it.

**Architectural transformation:** instead of asking the planner-shaped question “which backlog item ranks first?”, the agent uses the rejected authority boundary and the ontology's **alternative → possible consequence → possible future** relation as **an invitation to examine who can formulate the original goals**.

> **Owner (fictional):** “Would an automatic ranking help me decide what to do next?”
>
> **Agent (fictional):** “The automatic selection role was explicitly rejected. Rather than imitate it, consider a different question: a new contributor can understand *why* a design was rejected, but can they meaningfully challenge *which user outcome mattered* when we rejected it?”
>
> **New contributor (fictional):** “I can read why the system must not choose the next task for us. What I cannot yet express is a different goal: I want to be able to ask a source-grounded question about what the product should do for someone like me, without that being mistaken for an approved engineering task.”
>
> **Owner (fictional):** “That would be a different thing to value from faster task choice—but I would need to know whether contributors actually want it.”

**Different beneficiary experience, if it were real:** rather than passively receiving an ordered recommendation or an architectural visualization, a participant may **originate a source-grounded but expressly non-authoritative challenge to the product's existing question**. The owner might encounter a previously unconsidered conception of worthwhile participation *before* any implementation decision. No interface, workflow, permission infrastructure or runtime is implied by the fictional dialogue.

**Why it might matter:** it could increase *the ability to formulate relevant aspirations*, not merely confidence in a selected architectural option. The prospective benefit concerns agency in problem formulation and the owner's ability to recognize neglected purposes. However, the benefit may be modest, undesirable or entirely duplicative of ordinary human discussion. An unauthorized contributor proposal must not become owner intent, source fact or construction authority.

**Novelty challenge:** **DE-B2** already describes participant model construction/challenge, **DE-A3** already identifies unasked questions, **DE-E1/E2** already consider learning judgment, **DE-F2/GD-D** already propose scenario-based experience rehearsal, and **NX-O3** imagines delegation-shaped proposal/review/decision distinctions. This experience is best read as a **REVISED combination with a sharper source-derived causal hinge**, not a demonstrated new product goal. A different beneficiary alone is not proof of distinct value.

**Consequence propagation:** if such encounters shifted which questions owners considered legitimate, future discussions might include outcomes not captured by task backlog entries. But they might also consume more attention, attract unsupported user-demand stories, overprivilege whoever can articulate a technically sourced proposal, or invite pressure to canonize speculative ideas. Evidence about actual contributor wants, participation permissions and owner behavior would be needed before claiming product value.

### Deliberate negative control: the owner-controlled future dashboard

A fictional experience in which the owner sees unranked architecture cards, chooses one, and asks the agent to explain the options would largely **RESTATE** [Generative Depth mechanism F](2026-11-02-generative-depth-repository-knowledge.md), existing agent-native architectural synthesis and curated possibility ecology. Different visual organization or a renamed planner is neither evidence of a new capability nor of new user ends.

**Why this control matters:** if the inquiry cannot explain a difference in *what the beneficiary can want, question or accomplish*—as distinct from the design of the answer—it should report **RESTATED** and stop, not invent another interface family.

## 4. Direct, second-order and systemic consequences

The following are conditional implications of the **one** creative transformation, not empirical effects of current Sensemaking. For each chain the possible mechanism, affected person and stopping condition matter more than how many branches are written.

| Layer | Consequence, mechanism and beneficiary | Epistemic status; break/counterexample |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Direct — comparison without selection** | The agent can **compare reasons and hypothetical consequences** while leaving strategic authority untouched; the owner receives questions rather than a highest-ranked next task. | **Entailed distinction under accepted authority** that comparison ≠ automatic selection; usefulness of the questions is **UNPROVEN**. A disguised ranker would violate the premise. |
| **Direct — role of rejection changes** | Rejection history is consulted not only as a veto on implementing a planner but as **material for asking which human need or agency boundary motivated the veto**. | **Plausible source-guided creative move**, closely anticipated by GD-F. It may yield an already known value. |
| **Second-order — goal can precede structure** | A conditional participant challenge can make the owner consider **legitimacy of originating questions**, not merely correctness of choosing among tasks. | **Creative hypothesis**; requires a beneficiary who actually finds this meaningful. DE-B2 and DE-A3 are strong prior neighbors. |
| **Second-order — structure can precede a new goal hypothesis** | Reinterpreting the originally rejected comparator's relationship can reveal an *unasked beneficiary question* before the owner has articulated a new product end. | **Possible order reversal**, not demonstrated goal invention. An ordinary source-only reader might do the same. |
| **Systemic — who sets the agenda** | Even if automatic selection is prohibited, the **agent still influences which candidate questions and alternatives the owner ever sees**. Creative exaptation could broaden that agenda—or quietly narrow it to historically indexed concepts. | **Unexpected governance/creativity interaction**; avoiding the last automatic decision is not enough to guarantee unbiased *question selection*. No new approval stage is prescribed. |
| **Systemic — knowledge feedback** | If some hypothetical participant questions become source-backed decisions, later memory may amplify them and make further similar questions salient; unarticulated needs may remain unseen. | **Plausible feedback**, related to PR #719's memory-homogenization hypothesis; no demonstrated effect. |
| **Systemic — optionality versus burden** | Preserving histories of declined structures can invite alternative ends and conditional experiences, but retaining *every* future for consideration can overload the owner and leave decisions perpetually unsettled. | **Plausible two-sided effect**; no universal obligation to store, display or revive all variants. |
| **Unexpected — rejected authority becomes experience content** | The reason a planner was forbidden could itself become material for a participant's experience of **legitimate question authorship** instead of automation of someone else's judgment. | **Conditional conceptual transformation**, not a new code component or proved product outcome. |

### Beneficial/harmful interactions, not generic risk inventory

**Possible positive loop:** an agent presents the *reason* a historical design was rejected → an owner or contributor asks a different product question → that question identifies a meaningful beneficiary outcome → source qualification keeps the emerging idea separate from accepted work → the owner has a broader set of legitimate futures to consider. **Critical break:** neither meaningful demand nor future value follows solely from a creative exchange.

**Possible harmful loop:** a source-rich architectural comparison makes one historically privileged question appear unusually sophisticated → the agent supplies vivid hypothetical beneficiaries to justify it → the owner treats the invented need as discovery → future knowledge cites the speculative scenario repeatedly → apparently rigorous precedent crowds out unrepresented experiences. **Critical boundary:** source provenance of the *starting design decision* cannot validate an imaginary participant's desire.

**Counterintuitive duality:** the same no-automatic-planner boundary can **protect final strategic authority** and still leave **upstream agenda-setting influence** in the agent's hands. If creativity becomes a way to determine what is visible for consideration, the epistemic limits of that visibility may matter more than whether a ranking button exists. This adds an important consequence without implying a planner, governance engine or mandatory panel of reviewers.

## 8. Remaining unknowns, local diminishing returns and stop

**Questions this conceptual record cannot answer:**

1. Would a real owner or contributor perceive **question authorship** as meaningfully different and valuable compared with ordinary source-grounded explanation or design consultation?
2. Could an independent coding agent, unprompted, perform this specific exaptation? Would a source-only agent reading ADR 0029 and the Idea Library do as well as one with reviewed semantic relations?
3. Could an alternative architectural seed outside the rejected planner reveal a genuinely different *experience class* beyond this already crowded conceptual neighborhood?
4. Where is the boundary between inviting legitimate hypotheses from a new participant and wrongly treating that participant's proposal as owner intent or consent?
5. Does presenting more alternative futures increase owner agency or merely transfer hidden selection power to the agent's choice of questions?
6. Can the **rejection-reason → beneficiary-experience** relation become reusable across genuine later sessions without incentivizing novelty laundering? No cross-session evidence is in the inspected record.

**Local completion judgment:** one architectural relationship has been traced through a source-grounded premise, a full hypothetical beneficiary encounter, direct/second-order/systemic effects, beneficial/harmful loops, counterfactuals and nearest-predecessor comparison. Its strongest material consequence is the **agenda-setting paradox**: forbidding automatic action selection does not remove the agent's influence over which *product questions* reach the owner. Its strongest negative result is **no source-warranted genuinely new product goal**: the concrete participation-oriented experience closely refines prior DE/GD possibilities.

More speculative variations of this **same planner** premise now risk repeating GD-F, DE-B2 and the established Idea Library. Additional intellectual depth would need a materially different source relationship, beneficiary evidence, or a genuinely unexamined consequence—not a new dashboard metaphor. This is **local** diminishing returns only, not a claim that architectural exaptation or the product direction is exhausted. Empirical/owner qualification is distinct and **not initiated**.
