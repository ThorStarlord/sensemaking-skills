## 3. Comparable capability directions: purpose, distinctiveness and tradeoffs

**A — Recognize purpose-misaligned intellectual progress (M01 / M02 / M09).**  
**Benefit:** catch a technically correct, polished and source-cited answer that nevertheless does not advance the question the current inquiry is supposed to serve. M01 judges the *answer-to-purpose relationship*; M02 reconstructs why the current question was asked and whether its premise still holds; M09 compares the **kind of progress** actually made against the intellectual operation. These are complementary lenses rather than substitutes.  
**Distinctive application:** the crosswalk already warns against local optimization replacing product discovery, but does not demonstrate a reliable, low-cost way to notice quiet drift *while* an inquiry looks productive. **False positive:** treating evidence accumulation, narrow verification or a deliberately bounded task as unproductive merely because it yields no new product idea. **False negative:** fluent, informative outputs can conceal a slowly displaced inquiry target. **Cost/uncertainty:** repeated purpose checks could become ceremony; what precise observation justifies review before task closure remains a focused depth question. Can work transiently using existing frame/output records; no scoring or new state implied.

**B — Attribute an inadequate outcome to the right cause (M05 / M08; M06 conditional).**  
**Benefit:** prevent the agent from rewriting a sound reasoning method when the true problem is misread evidence, a bad frame, unavailable specialist guidance, a host permission conflict or an external dependency. The key is M05's rival *explanations of the same observed inadequacy*. M08 protects the delivery/availability boundary; M06 can ask whether a materially different method would discriminate or help, without treating that hypothetical advantage as an observed win.  
**Distinctiveness:** the existing abductive ontology already represents the mechanism; the specificity of *method-versus-question-versus-host* attribution is an under-specified **application**, not a new representation need. **False positive:** interpret a single unsatisfying result as verified method failure. **False negative:** focus entirely on environment/tool access and miss a genuine question mismatch. **Cost/uncertainty:** high-overhead differential diagnosis is disproportionate when direct source correction resolves the issue; evidence that distinguishes internal method inadequacy from missing guidance remains limited to observable acts, not private cognition.

**C — Revise or compose methods in proportion to the problem (M06 / M07 / M17).**  
**Benefit:** preserve a valid question while correcting one inference, changing one lens, adopting another primary operation, combining complementary operations, or restoring the original method. M07 concerns the **smallest correction to one method**; M17 concerns **the relationship among several valid methods** whose ordering or scope can fail collectively; M06 offers a cheap counterfactual alternative.  
**Distinctiveness:** the controller already handles adaptive composition and the policies already support method changes. The incremental opportunity is diagnosing the *unit* of the error (local method versus composition), not adding a pipeline. **False positive:** elaborate method orchestration when one source read would suffice. **False negative:** repeatedly repairing an individual specialist while the handoff/sequencing across specialists is the actual problem. **Cost/uncertainty:** multi-method analysis can balloon. Existing specialist-return and policy deactivation mechanisms reduce this risk; their effectiveness in real composition mismatches is unqualified.

**D — Correct an inadequate inquiry or evolve a valid one (M03 / M04 / M18).**  
**Benefit:** preserve both **corrective reframing** (M03: old question/frame inadequate) and **productive evolution** (M18: old question still valid; its sound answer suggests a *different consequential inquiry*). M04 can surface source relationships and unexpected consequences from which either question might emerge.  
**Distinctiveness:** `revisesQuestion`, `opensQuestion` and `TransformationAccount` already express different histories; the difficult intellectual step is deciding which history is true and whether the additional question serves accepted purpose. **False positive:** promoting a fascinating tangent, or labelling a successful original question retrospectively wrong. **False negative:** missing a new consequential question because the first question was answered correctly. **Cost/uncertainty:** unbounded question branching is especially dangerous for high-delegation agents. Continued inquiry, changed goals and protected product commitments must remain separately warranted and authorized.

**E — Preserve consequential continuity and learn without universalizing (M12 / M13; M02 / M18 when material).**  
**Benefit:** retain the original question, evidence, valid findings, changed assumption and reason for a consequential revision (M12); separately retain a **context-limited lesson** about method fitness across episodes when repeated evidence supports it (M13).  
**Distinctiveness:** existing inquiry/frame revision, provenance, receipts/handoffs and Learning / Reconciliation already represent the meaningful distinctions. This is a *durability complement*, not an independent way to discover a method mismatch. **False positive:** store every transitory branch and promote an anecdote to a universal method rule. **False negative:** lose enough predecessor evidence that a returning agent repeats a failed search or mistakes a changed question for an original one. **Cost/uncertainty:** selective retention is inexpensive conceptually but retrieval/maintenance can dominate; current evidence does not show reliable cross-context benefit.

### Cross-direction synthesis, not a feature ranking

| Question of comparison | Strongest **plausible intellectual role** | Why another direction is not a substitute | What would change this judgment |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Notice apparently successful but irrelevant work before closure** | **A**, with D's purpose-sensitive question contrast | B mainly diagnoses causes *after* an inadequacy is noticed; C revises methods once a correction is warranted | Authentic countercases showing existing global/local orientation already catches drift, or M09 introduces frequent false alarms |
| **Avoid wrongly attributing failure to the reasoning method** | **B**, with M16's observable evidence limits | A can flag mismatch but does not identify the cause; C is an intervention, not diagnosis | Source-qualified real case showing a simpler evidence correction resolves the ambiguity or repeated misattribution despite B |
| **Change the reasoning approach without discarding good work** | **C**, supported by E | D may change a question when the method is already suitable; C must not automatically change a valid question | Genuine multi-operation case where local correction fails but composition change succeeds (or the reverse) |
| **Discover consequential new questions through correct reasoning** | **D**, grounded by A/M01 and preserved by E/M12 | A can notice relevance but does not itself generate a new question; B's failure diagnosis is sometimes inapplicable | Case where new-question identification is reliably indistinguishable from post-hoc novelty/tangent |
| **Make later agents reconstruct consequential inquiry changes** | **E** | A–D can work transiently; none guarantees a later reader can recover changed premises | Demonstrated loss of important method history, or ordinary existing records suffice without dedicated retention |

**Provisional interpretive preference (for a *future conceptual inquiry only*):** **A and D jointly expose the most consequential underexplored distinction in this corpus**—a well-executed but purpose-misaligned inquiry versus a well-executed inquiry that *successfully opens* a more important question. This is not a claim that A/D are implemented, causally effective, better than the current controller, or two architecture modules to build. **B remains the chief diagnostic safeguard** when genuine inadequacy is observed; **C is conditional intervention**; **E supports continuity**. Their lack of commensurable outputs makes an overall winner inappropriate.

## 4. Composition: additional capability without an automatic pipeline

One **hypothetical** composite is: M01/M09 notice a material purpose mismatch; M05 distinguishes rival causes; M08 checks access/host delivery where indicated; M06 compares one alternative method if decision-changing; M07 adjusts one method *or* M17 changes composition; M12 preserves valid old findings; M10/M11 constrain needless switching and reflection; M15 protects ratified intent; M16 examines visible output/trace. **This is a set of conditional relationships, not a mandated sequence.**

Three meaningfully different **partial compositions** show why smaller may be better:

- **Clear narrow task, no mismatch:** retain the question and method; direct authorized work and bounded verification. M10/M11 prevent a pointless diagnostic pass.
- **Repeated unsuccessful engineering analysis with uncertain cause:** use M05 to distinguish source error, unsuitable method, bad question, and host issue; only then choose a locally sufficient correction (M07) or operation-composition change (M17). An M06 alternative is an inquiry possibility, not a second compulsory analysis.
- **Accurate technical answer exposes a new product-contract question:** M18 with M04 and M01 may preserve the answered technical question while opening a separate provisional inquiry. M12 preserves source-qualified findings; M15 prevents silently replacing owner goals. No failure diagnosis is needed.

**Composition risks and counterweights:** M09 may trigger on legitimate repetitive verification (M10); M05's rival lists may manufacture explanations without evidence (S5); M17 may turn a simple task into a workflow (M11); two agents may share anchoring and reinforce error (M14 is optional); M18 may create agenda drift (M01/M15 plus Warrant/Choice); M12/M13 may produce administrative memory rather than helpful continuity (existing retention threshold); Skill loading may be mistaken for substantive reasoning (M08/M16). None requires an all-encompassing process or metareasoning database.

## 5. M18: distinguish correction, productive evolution and a tangent

The decisive object of comparison is not whether a second question is *interesting* but what it changes, why it is legitimate, and whether the first question remains sound.

| Situation | What is true about the original inquiry? | Treatment of the later question | Appropriate evidence and boundary |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **1. Corrective reframing (M03)** | An earlier premise/frame is insufficient, too narrow, misdirected or no longer fit for its governing purpose; valid individual facts may survive | Consider a revised local question/frame, citing the exact changed assumption and retaining the predecessor | Evidence connecting the original miss to purpose or scope, not mere novelty; never rewrite the earlier question's history or owner intent |
| **2. Productive question evolution (M18)** | The earlier inquiry is valid and its answer remains useful; source results disclose a distinct, possibly more consequential question | Preserve the successful original answer, record a new *provisional* question and separately judge its decision relevance/authority | An identifiable source-to-question conceptual change; what new decision/beneficiary it could affect; uncertainty and opportunity cost; no automatic continuation |
| **3. Interesting but non-consequential tangent** | The earlier inquiry remains valid; the generated curiosity has no sufficiently grounded link to current purpose or decision | Note briefly or discard without expanding work, unless a future independently warranted inquiry emerges | No demonstrated decision-changing benefit; preserving an imaginative aside does not make it a product priority |

**Illustration (a conceptual case, not new runtime evidence):** An agent correctly explains how a previously ended Ticket may resume after policy revalidation. That answer can reveal the distinct owner-level question of which historical authorizations *ought* to survive policy changes amid multiple Executors. This is situation 2 if its decision relevance can be articulated, not an admission that the technical inquiry was invalid. A speculative new UI for monitoring every Ticket might instead be situation 3 if nothing connects it to the user's decision. See the [source-grounded same-context DSH observation](../normal-use/2026-11-07-dsh-followup-same-context-application-observation.md); that observation is not an independent demonstration of metareasoning causation.

The key **false inference** is "new question discovered => old question was wrong" or "new question discovered => new goal selected." `opensQuestion` is not `revisesQuestion`, and neither means `RatifiedIntent` or implementation authority. [Divergent Exploration ontology](../semantic-architecture/divergent-exploration-ontology.md#3-relationship-meanings) and [abductive-to-divergent semantics](../semantic-architecture/evidence-grounded-abductive-divergent-ontology-v1.md#3-documentary-relations-and-establishment) can represent this already.

### Governing principles

1. **It is not selecting the most elaborate reasoning mechanism; it is identifying which intellectual uncertainty could most improve judgment.**
2. **It is not a mandatory pipeline and it is not a universal ranking; it is a conditional composition of distinct responsibilities with an explicit stop.**
3. **Productive metareasoning is knowing when a successful question should be preserved, an inadequate one corrected, and a consequential new one opened without inventing authority.**
