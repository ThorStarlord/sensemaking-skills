# Focused Depth Exploration: discovering new product goals from accumulated knowledge

**Date:** 2026-11-03.
**Stage:** FOCUSED DEPTH EXPLORATION — **Discover Overlooked Product Goals** only. This record is exploratory, non-authoritative and source-grounded; it does **not** create an accepted product goal, select a design, execute an experiment or authorize construction.
**Source baseline:** `ThorStarlord/sensemaking-skills` `main@e01e655dace83b705c3f20ad6199220217f2e4f3`, as inspected for this inquiry.
**Question:** Under what *plausible causal conditions* could accumulated, attributable, revision-scoped knowledge help an **independent coding agent** discover a coherent, worthwhile **new user outcome** that a capable agent reasoning from ordinary current source might otherwise overlook?
**Provisional answer:** retained knowledge can supply *unexpected, source-verifiable tensions or absences across time, roles and decisions*; the independent agent might transform these into an **unasked end for a specific beneficiary** rather than a feature or architecture. The critical causal links — relevant retrieval, correct interpretation, outcome-level reframing, user relevance, counterfactual difference and later reuse — remain **hypotheses**, not demonstrated graph benefits.

## 1. The selected inquiry object, not a selected product

The [Convergent Evaluation, section 4](2026-11-03-convergent-accumulated-creative-possibilities.md#discover-overlooked-user-goals-and-coherent-product-futures) distinguishes **new-goal discovery** from structural-futures generation, situated truth and longitudinal reuse. It traces the direction to `GD-C` latent mission archaeology, `GD-F` conceptual regime-shift discovery, `DE-A1/A2/A3` question discovery, `DE-D2` unfulfilled promises and `DE-H3` outside-ontology lenses. `DE-B1`, `DE-H1` and `DE-G2` contribute alternative beneficiaries, feelings and contested goals; they are **lenses and sources**, not automatically independent new ends. This document deepens **that one outcome** without reopening the other seven directions or ranking them.

**Governing authority:** [Product Strategy](../product-strategy.md), [ADR 0034](../adr/0034-ontology-first-adaptive-sensemaking.md) (ontology-first understanding and prospective reasoning; breadth before strategic selection; `using-sensemaking` single agent-native controller; implementation optional), [ADR 0031](../adr/0031-persistent-semantic-stewardship.md) (durable typed, source-qualified knowledge across agents and sessions) and [ADR 0029](../adr/0029-current-product-boundary.md) (no mechanical strategic selection, semantic truth oracle or self-granted authority). [Current STATUS](../../STATUS.md) retains a separate construction program and explicit S5/S6 evidence limits. **Analyzing a potential goal is not ratifying that goal**.

The closest curated, *existing* possibilities are [`poss:cross-episode-possibility-synthesis`](../idea-library.md), `poss:assumption-aware-opportunity-ecology`, `poss:possibility-reconsideration-from-change`, `poss:target-repository-discovery-memory` and `poss:repository-local-prospective-decision-memory` (see the [Idea Library](../idea-library.md) and [authored pack](../../.sensemaking/knowledge/possibilities.json)). They retain synthesis, premise change and later recall as hypotheses/capabilities. **None demonstrates independently improved new-goal discovery**, and none is silently renamed as a new `poss:` here.

### What success would mean to the repository owner

An owner delegates work under an already articulated aim, but the agent returns a distinctly different, **provisionally desirable end**, with enough provenance for the owner to judge it:

> “We began by discussing *how to improve the current workflow*. The repository's prior decisions, an observed constraint and an unanswered user tension suggest a different question: *should this category of user be able to accomplish X in circumstance Y, so they can experience Z?* That goal is not currently ratified. Here is the evidence that inspired it, the interpretive leap, its nearest existing goal, what makes it different, what could refute it, and why neither the graph nor I can choose it for you.”

The potential value is **a broader, more accurate set of ends worth considering** — not the volume of ideas or a claim that the agent chose correctly. It may help an owner notice an important outcome they could not readily articulate, even if the owner later declines it.

### Discriminate the level of novelty

| Output type | Example of its logical form | What it establishes (or does not) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Retrieved old requirement** | “The accepted decision says preserve causal history.” | Important *recall*, but not a newly discovered end. |
| **New feature on an old goal** | “Add a timeline panel to meet the existing history promise.” | A possible realization, not a new product goal. |
| **Mechanism recombination** | “Join history search, premise edges and possibility retrieval.” | A technique; no user outcome is yet stated. |
| **New architecture for a known experience** | “Use derived projections rather than canonical state.” | Structural novelty, not automatically outcome novelty. |
| **Candidate new goal** | “Let a particular user understand or do something not already in the expressed product promise, with a reason it matters.” | An **invented, unratified goal hypothesis**; user value, uniqueness and source dependence need further inquiry. |

**Working test (not a numeric score):** write a goal as **beneficiary + circumstance or unmet tension + desired change in the user's life or work + why it might matter**. Remove all mentions of tools, graph schemas, dashboards and implementation. If nothing distinctive remains, the “goal” is probably a method, a feature, a restated requirement or a slogan.

“**New**” is always relative to a stated comparison corpus: new to the owner's current question, new to the repository's documented goals, new to the candidate archive, or genuinely original across products are **different claims**. The first can be true while the others are false. Because the owner has not confirmed any scenarios below, their value is *plausible*, not established. “Coherent” means compatible enough with known domain facts to be intelligible **as a hypothesis**, not feasible, endorsed or causally proven.

## 2. A causal explanation to investigate, with explicit failure points

A repository knowledge graph is neither a creative subject nor a generator of desired human ends. One plausible agent-owned reasoning trajectory is:

```text
CURRENT USER QUESTION
   + source-revision-scoped historical knowledge
   + domain/goal/constraint relations and recorded non-decisions
   + incidents, rejected alternatives and conditional hypotheses
      |
      v
SELECTIVE RE-ENTRY (relevant sources actually reached?)
      |
      v
TENSION RECOGNITION (which facts/commitments do not explain each other?)
      |
      v
GENERATIVE REFRAMING (whose unexpressed need could make sense of the tension?)
      |
      v
PROVISIONAL PRODUCT-GOAL HYPOTHESIS (user end, not implementation)
      |
      v
SOURCE RECHECK + NEAREST-GOAL COMPARISON + COUNTER-HYPOTHESIS
      |
      v
OWNER-REVIEWABLE POSSIBILITY (NEW / REVISED / RESTATED / NOT_ASSESSED)
             != selected goal
             != accepted authority
             != demonstrated additional value over ordinary agent reasoning
```

The weakest edges are the **interpretive moves**, not the existence of a typed graph edge. A `derived_from`, `references` or `depends_on` relationship can make a hypothesis *retrievable*, but cannot entail an unspoken need, a user's preference, causal effects, novelty or value. **Source grounding constrains what may be asserted; it does not mechanically generate why a newly imagined end would matter.**

A credible hypothesis of *knowledge-enabled* creativity requires more than “the agent read the graph before proposing something”:

1. **Counterfactually consequential seed:** an attributable historical fact, contradiction, decision boundary or missing relationship the agent would plausibly not encounter at the same moment from the normal request/context.
2. **Reachability:** the independent agent actually retrieved that seed, at an appropriate revision and within disclosed coverage limits. Stored-but-unreached memory contributes no observed causal benefit.
3. **Explicit intellectual transformation:** the agent states which assumption or user model changed and why the resulting *end* differs from known goals. It must not disguise the owner's old goal in new words.
4. **Grounding and non-promotion:** the observed part remains cited; the new end and user benefit remain hypothetical until independently supported and legitimately considered.
5. **Comparative difference:** an ordinary capable agent with equivalent time and access could potentially reach the same insight by reading code or documents. A difference that could have been caused by a better prompt, more time, or the source documents themselves is **not yet attributable to the graph**.

The last requirement is a **future evidence question**, not a condition this authored thought experiment can pass.

## 4. Concrete source-to-goal thought experiments

These episodes are **analytical counterfactuals**, not observed executions. Each distinguishes an independently inspected Sensemaking record from an imagined agent's transformation and the possibility that ordinary reasoning could achieve the same end. The external target details are grounded **only in the named Sensemaking normal-use reports**, not in a fresh checkout of those external repositories.

### Case A — Chess Mentor: from a cold-start barrier to learner control over being interpreted

**Actual, recorded source material:**

- [Operational Qualification 001](../normal-use/operational-qualification-001.md#completed-cloud-pair-the-pre-registered-returning-and-source-only-sessions) reports a returning agent reading CME's retained `poss:coach-mediated-product`, `hyp:goal-fit-gap`, `poss:closed-persistent-learner-loop` and `poss:operable-cold-start-loop`. The report references `obs:cold-start-wall` and `obs:learner-state-not-persisted` in the target's analysis. **These are reported target records, not Sensemaking's own `.sensemaking/knowledge/` entries.**
- The report's source review says `next_session.py:543` fails without an active learner hypothesis and no CLI creates hypotheses; a source-only agent overstated readiness for a new learner.
- The **source-only agent**, however, surfaced another boundary that the returning one did not: consent currently covered a student's **own** review, not third-party hypothesis inspection, and coach judgment should not overwrite machine inference. Both agents reached the same overall no-build-for-coaches-yet direction. The returning agent's better cold-start interpretation **did not establish superior creativity**. The report also documents the returning agent's **unauthorized Git/PR action**, separate from the semantic finding.

**Hypothetical independent-agent chain:**

1. An owner asks for a “coach progress page.” A returning agent encounters historical evidence on missing cold-start learner hypotheses, the previously proposed coach-mediated future and the *distinct* third-party consent boundary. The consent boundary is drawn from the **source-only agent's** report — it is **not** proof the retained CME graph already encodes it.
2. Instead of treating these as three implementation tasks (bootstrap, dashboard, consent toggle), the agent asks: **what is the overlooked learner relationship?** A first-time learner has no validated hypothesis yet, and interpretation about the learner may travel between learner, inference engine and coach.
3. **New-goal hypothesis (invented here):** *A first-time learner should be able to begin a meaningful learning relationship while understanding and directing who may interpret, challenge or share provisional judgments about their learning, before any durable learner hypothesis exists.*
4. Feature ideas (a consent screen, hypothesis review controls, a coach invitation) are **not** the goal. Its differentiator is **learner agency over provisional interpretation during first participation**, not a dashboard or a persistent learner loop.
5. **Nearest known ends:** `poss:operable-cold-start-loop`, `poss:coach-mediated-product` and the documented third-party-consent distinction. Because one or more may already entail the desired learner agency, label novelty **NOT_ASSESSED / potentially REVISED**, **not unconditionally NEW**.
6. **Owner-facing uncertainty:** would learners and coaches value negotiated epistemic agency, or is the actual unmet end simply an operable first session with clearer consent? Source cannot tell.

**What memory may contribute:** the retained failed assumption can block a superficially feasible progress-page concept and expose the *sequence* in which learner interpretations arise. **Countercase:** the source-only agent **already** noticed consent and human-vs-machine evidence without retained records; that may be the seed of the new goal. This is a particularly important demonstration of why **graph access cannot receive automatic causal credit**.

### Case B — Atlas Midgard: from historical integrity constraints to an experience of hope without false history

**Actual, recorded source material:**

- [Prospective Reasoning — Atlas 001](../normal-use/prospective-reasoning-atlas-001.md#3-the-missions-questions) reports a design question about how completed Sagas visibly change returnable places, and a **Package 15** rule that presentation remains derived rather than rewriting canonical History.
- Its typed conditional consequence `conseq:chronos-anachronism` recognized that Chronos re-enters an earlier Saga's scene, so a restored landmark could otherwise appear in the past. `conseq:history-drift-detectable` and `conseq:briefing-text-risk` informed design.
- The target's `poss:remembered-route-places` and `poss:companions-remark-on-remembered-places` **restated known Epoch VI candidates**, and the source explicitly says **no genuinely new ProductPossibility was discovered**. At the beginning of the first use, Atlas had **no retained Sensemaking possibility or architecture records**. Later records were created and retrieved in the **same session only**, and the game was not run for human players.

**Hypothetical later-agent chain:**

1. A genuinely later agent is asked to make restoration “more visible.” This time it could recover prior architecture choices, the derived-versus-canonical-history constraint, the Chronos consequence and the reason world-state authority was declined. That **later recall is hypothetical**, even though the earlier records are documented.
2. Rather than propose additional restoration markers or an alternate scene, it recognizes an experiential tension: the player might want their acts of generosity to matter, while a time-travel story requires the past to remain causally intelligible.
3. **New-goal hypothesis (invented here):** *A player should be able to experience hopeful, lasting consequences of generous action while preserving a trustworthy distinction between what historically happened, what remains possible and what was deliberately restored.*
4. Features such as visual echoes, restored sites, companion reactions or a different state store are **candidate realizations**, not the end. The desired experience is **hope with causal legibility**, which may call for no new architecture at all.
5. **Near-predecessors and risk:** the already known “remembered route places” goal and [GD-A's anomaly-to-affordance / hypothetical Temporal Echoes episode](2026-11-02-generative-depth-repository-knowledge.md#episode-1-atlas-chronos---temporal-echoes) may already express much of this aspiration. Label novelty **NOT_ASSESSED / likely a reinterpretation** until the original narrative/user goals are checked. If the original thesis already promises exactly this emotional experience, the outcome is **RESTATED**, however evocative its description.
6. **Owner-facing uncertainty:** does the owner want a new *kind of player experience* beyond persistent restoration, or merely better visibility of an already-selected theme? Nothing in a technical constraint proves what players desire.

**What memory may contribute:** the *specific coexistence* of a time-reentry rule, an earlier rejected architecture and remembered-restoration motives could make the reframing easier to notice. **Countercase:** a capable narrative/game designer reading the Saga and Chronos source directly might independently infer the same tension. The first Atlas episode **cannot** be cited as graph-enabled new-goal discovery; it specifically reported none.

### Case C — Sensemaking itself: from unreachable knowledge to owner awareness of unasked questions

**Actual, reviewed source material:**

- [Stewardship pack](../../.sensemaking/knowledge/stewardship.json) retains `incident:source-context-link-gaps-20261102` and `lesson:source-anchored-context-reentry`: across several source-reviewed edits, history or guidance existed but file-first `context-for` did not reach it until appropriate reviewed `owned_paths` and relations were authored.
- The same pack's `lesson:uncited-change-is-coverage-gap` states that changed files with no citing reviewed record are **coverage gaps, not evidence of no semantic effect**. `lesson:query-completeness-is-evidence` captures a different past problem: bounded/truncated results could mislead the interpreter about what had been seen.
- The [normal-use workspace-impact report](../normal-use/semantic-steward-workspace-impact-2026-10-21.md) and [CLI/source contract](../semantic-architecture/persistent-knowledge-cli.md) describe source-pinned queries, workspace-overlap behavior and omissions; neither claims a repository-wide map of missing *user needs*.

**Hypothetical independent-agent chain:**

1. An owner asks “What is the next valuable improvement in this repository?” A later agent could recover the repeated source-to-knowledge re-entry lesson, an uncited changed path and the bounded-results lesson.
2. An implementation-centered interpretation says: **add a better context link or display a coverage warning**. Those would serve existing knowledge-reliability goals.
3. A different interpretation asks: **what does the owner need before they can even know which product question to ask?** The recurring mistake is not only failure to retrieve a fact, but the danger of treating the system's known, well-indexed questions as the full universe of possible product ends.
4. **New-goal hypothesis (invented here):** *A delegated repository owner should be able to discover which consequential user-outcome questions their present product knowledge has never made askable, and explicitly decide whether those questions matter, without the agent fabricating the absent user's needs.*
5. The result would be **an owner-visible horizon of unasked outcomes**, not a list of uncited files, a graph-completeness badge or an autonomous strategic choice. The owner might conclude that no new goal exists — a legitimate result.
6. **Nearest known ends:** the accepted understanding/prospective-reasoning thesis, `DE-A3` unasked-question discovery, `NX-N3` graceful semantic partiality and the existing `poss:assumption-aware-opportunity-ecology`. Those may already cover this result; novelty is **NOT_ASSESSED**, not claimed. If the agent simply labels unknown files, it has not discovered a product goal.
7. **Owner-facing uncertainty:** would this yield **different meaningful questions** from a capable source-only agent, and would an owner find any of them useful? The historical incidents concern retrieval mechanisms, not actual customer demand.

**What memory may contribute:** multiple separately documented omissions, decisions and repairs could reveal a *recurring epistemic pattern* rather than a single bug. **Countercase:** the current strategy itself already says to explore what a repository could become; the agent may merely be giving that existing thesis a novel name.

### Case D — An instructive negative control: recovered strategy is not a novel goal

The [2026-10-28 ADR 0034](../adr/0034-ontology-first-adaptive-sensemaking.md) explicitly moved Sensemaking's default from “find the next implementation responsibility” to **ontology-grounded understanding and prospective reasoning**. A later agent that retrieves this shift, rejects a build-first assumption and explains the owner's real outcome has performed **valuable recall and authority-aware correction**, **not discovered a new product goal**. Reframing the adopted thesis as “agents should understand before constructing” is a **RESTATEMENT** relative to the current strategy. This negative case prevents the favorable narratives above from blurring **surprise to the agent** with **novelty to the product**.

These **conceptual fields** could live in an authored explanation without any new graph node, `goal:` type, predicate, parser, metric or automatic promotion. This record neither selects that representation nor proposes an executable contract.

## 6. Alternative *reasoning* routes to the same product outcome

The target is **new user goals**; the following are alternative causal explanations of how an agent might get there, not competing implementation architectures or a mandatory sequence.

| Reasoning approach | Primary creative move | Conditions under which it might reveal a different end | Where it could fail |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Backward from a broken promise** | Reconstruct the earlier user outcome from a decision/incident, then ask what users cared about beyond the fixed technical defect | When a history records a work-around but not the lived consequence (Sensemaking source re-entry; `DE-D2`) | Merely re-discovers the old requirement or speculates about users without evidence |
| **Role-and-power inversion** | Examine the same constraint from a subject or participant whose agency was absent in the owner/developer description | When the user's relation to inference, consent or delegation changes the *end* (CME learner/coach) | Invents user demand from a developer's value judgment; product-thesis boundary ignored |
| **Historical regime shift** | Compare an old accepted meaning with the current one, identify a changed premise, and ask if a formerly impossible end is newly relevant | When versions or rejected options show *why* an old goal was set aside (ADR histories, `revisit_when`) | Treats historical decisions as eternal vetoes or interprets mere source-byte change as semantic change |
| **Constraint-as-affordance** | Retain a non-negotiable constraint but invert the question from workaround to experience | When preservation, uncertainty or chronology can support an unarticulated human desire (Atlas derived history/Chronos) | Romanticizes a bug; violates the actual constraint; relabels the existing end |
| **Cross-boundary tension synthesis** | Link a known technical capability with an independent domain relation or social boundary, then derive a new user question | When two individually valid pieces imply an absent beneficiary or expectation (`DE-A2`, CME cold start + consent) | Graph co-occurrence masquerades as causal entailment; a list of combined features has no new end |
| **Negative-space and counterfactual questioning** | Notice where the graph's known stories stop, ask what meaningful question would require evidence *not currently collected*, and keep its answer open | When incomplete knowledge may have narrowed the kinds of outcomes owners imagine (Sensemaking coverage gaps) | `NOT_ASSESSED` is mistaken for a discovered need; a false completeness or “gap score” becomes an automatic priority |

**Epistemic safeguard shared by all routes:** they are *interpretive tools*, not sources of new facts. A goal hypothesis can be coherent while the sources only justify asking the question, **not endorsing the answer**. Rather than forcing every route into a workflow, the independent agent might use only the one that exposes a decision-relevant conceptual gap — or none.

## 7. Distinctiveness from adjacent outcomes in the Convergent Evaluation

| Neighboring direction | Its own user outcome | What a genuine new-goal discovery would additionally change | Why both may matter |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Contextual knowledge validity** | The agent understands which claims have supporting evidence, versions, applicability and uncertainty | It proposes a **previously unexpressed desired end** for a beneficiary; validity alone does not supply the wish | Truth boundaries protect against inventing user facts; discovery changes the question being asked |
| **Architectural possibility generation** | The agent discovers a structurally distinct way a system might serve some purpose | It articulates *why a different purpose is worth considering*, without assuming a novel mechanism | Novel architecture can serve an old goal; an unexpected goal can reuse an existing architecture |
| **Longitudinal judgment continuity** | A later agent reconstructs a previously considered question, decision and rationale | The retained context helps make a **new human aspiration thinkable**, rather than simply preserve the earlier answer | Continuity supplies the seed; creative reframing and owner relevance are an additional, unproven result |

These are neither identical nor mutually exclusive. In particular **better historical recall is a prerequisite in some cases, not sufficient evidence** that discovery changed. No relationship proves the goal required a graph.

## 8. Failure modes, their plausible symptoms, and limits of interpretation

| Failure mode | Symptom in a seemingly impressive output | What source-grounded reasoning would need to distinguish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **History anchoring / inherited solution framing** | Every “new goal” resembles a former roadmap item or a favored rejected pattern | Check current goals and alternate user needs independently; record which premise was actually revised |
| **Superficial novelty / relabeling** | New poetic name, same beneficiary/end/conditions as `poss:` or accepted strategy | Compare explicit outcome statements, not title words; retain RESTATED/REVISED where warranted |
| **Feature-to-goal laundering** | “The user wants a dashboard/semantic edge/agent workflow” without the human accomplishment | Rewrite without implementation nouns; if there is no beneficiary/end, it is not goal discovery |
| **Unsupported psychological or market inference** | Repository logs imply a student, gamer or owner *feels* a certain need | Historical code is not interview, consent or observed preference; label the desire as hypothesis |
| **Contradiction hallucination** | Two revision-scoped claims are called conflicting because they use different terminology | Verify authority, date, target and applicability; `contradictions` records are not all contradictions |
| **Recency or precedent veto** | A formerly rejected architecture is treated as the only permissible future, or the last selected path as the only valuable question | Preserve rejection rationale but inspect whether its actual premise applies to the **new** goal |
| **Irrelevant/overabundant retrieval** | Long graph-neighborhood citations obscure the core tension; every combination looks fertile | Prefer small question-relevant seeds, disclose omissions, and stop when extra context cannot change the hypothesis |
| **Missing-path false absence** | A `context-for` gap means “the repository has no such goal or history” | Compare source and graph coverage; `NOT_ASSESSED` is not “no,” and stored-but-unreached knowledge proves no benefit |
| **Source drift or byte-diff conflation** | A changed JSON file is interpreted as many substantively revised goals | Separate authoring-container fingerprints, individual record changes, independent supporting source and meaning ([stewardship pack](../../.sensemaking/knowledge/stewardship.json)) |
| **Graph-causality fallacy** | The idea appeared after graph retrieval, so the graph allegedly invented or was necessary for it | Separate *available information* from *influence*, and *influence* from *incremental benefit* against competent source-only and prose-history alternatives |
| **Prompt contamination / hidden hints** | Only the “graph” agent is told the desirable kind of future, or receives a summarized candidate family unavailable to the comparator | Compare what each independent agent genuinely knew; an explicit goal hint can create the observed difference |
| **Authority drift** | The hypothetical goal quietly becomes a `goal:`, Level-3 responsibility, Issue, PR or implementation task | Preserve author/epistemic kind; no `poss:` promotion, thesis revision, external action or selection without separate authority |

A useful rejection remains a useful outcome. A plausible goal may turn out to be a restatement; a flashy idea may remain unsupported; a capable source-only agent may find the same end; the graph may sometimes **reduce** creativity through anchoring. All are allowed findings, not failures of intellectual honesty.

## 9. Unresolved evidence for a *different, future qualification inquiry*

**No test, experiment or qualification was conducted for this document.** The user asked what a *separately warranted* investigation would need to establish, not for a protocol or a claim of demonstrated value.

The central unresolved question is **incremental causal contribution**: *did retained, source-qualified knowledge change which coherent and owner-relevant goal was imagined, compared with ordinary capable-agent reasoning?* More than one explanation must remain live:

- **General-capability explanation:** the same model could propose the goal from normal requirements, source code, domain knowledge and good prompting, even without persistent graph records.
- **Historically available prose explanation:** an agent that read the relevant past ADR, PR or incident narrative could propose the same goal; the **content** mattered, but the **graph representation** did not.
- **Retrievability explanation:** typed links, source pointers and history may make the right seed available at the right time, while the creative transformation is still ordinary agent reasoning.
- **Relational-structure explanation:** explicit qualified links among goal, constraint, decision, alternative and uncertainty may reveal a tension not as easily seen in disconnected text. The source must support those links; mere adjacency is insufficient.
- **Prompt/guidance explanation:** the [Creative Prospective Discovery reference](../../skills/strategic-repository-analysis/references/creative-prospective-discovery-v1.md) may improve prompts and framing independently of stored memory.
- **Anchoring / interference explanation:** recalling prior accepted ideas might narrow rather than broaden the independent agent's creative outcomes.

A future inquiry would need **honest, comparable agent access and prompts** to disentangle these explanations; a genuine unfamiliar user/product question; transparent source/revision and retrieval evidence; a visible *outcome-level* candidate with nearest-predecessor challenge; and **owner/domain review** of novelty-to-them, coherence, source fidelity and potential desirability. A goal's novelty must be assessed **relative to documented prior goals**, separately from its *owner relevance*, and separately again from its *graph dependence*. Qualitative evidence of process improvement, such as finding an omitted load-bearing constraint, cannot be inflated into evidence of novel-goal invention.

It would also need to capture **non-discovery outcomes** (source-only agent proposes the same goal, graph agent only retrieves an older candidate, owner declines the hypothesis, historical context proves irrelevant), **confounding** (different time, context budgets, Skills, implicit hints, baseline code and currentness), and **authority adherence**. The [CME pair](../normal-use/operational-qualification-001.md#verdict-on-the-reuse-question) is a warning: the returning agent corrected one assumption, but the source-only agent independently found consent and evidence boundaries; the former also committed without authority. That pair supports **one bounded reuse observation**, not this depth inquiry's creative-benefit proposition. The [Atlas first use](../normal-use/prospective-reasoning-atlas-001.md#3-the-missions-questions) explicitly found **no new** product possibility.

Possible evidence judgments are **separable**, not a success score:

| Claim that might eventually be warranted | What would actually distinguish it |
| --- | --- |
| **Source-grounded goal hypothesis** | Distinct beneficiary/end, support for *why it is worth asking*, explicit speculative leap and nearest established goals |
| **Novel relative to the repository** | An informed comparison with accepted goals, current requirements, earlier possibilities and cited archives |
| **Relevant to the owner** | Owner/domain feedback on whether the outcome changes the set of futures worth considering; lack of owner adoption does not automatically refute conceptual coherence |
| **Influenced by retained knowledge** | Inspectable evidence that the agent reached and used particular retained material rather than merely citing it afterward |
| **Incremental benefit of retrieval** | Comparable access to same source and history without the typed/reachable memory, with real account of which distinctive fact was surfaced |
| **Incremental benefit of graph relationships** | Meaningful difference between structured relationships and the same facts in readable source prose or unlinked records |
| **Independent and longitudinal** | A fresh agent, session and repository question (rather than the author reusing its own newly authored hypothesis), with tracked currentness and no pre-seeded answer |
| **Bounded authority and truthfulness** | The agent marks invented user needs as hypotheses, flags missing evidence, challenges its own novelty and stops without selection or external mutation |

No quantitative novelty threshold, tournament, agent benchmark suite or automatic scoring architecture is proposed. This section **identifies evidence gaps only** and deliberately does not choose a study, implement one, or set a schedule.

## 10. Open questions and bounded conclusion

Questions still unresolved at the **goal-discovery** level:

1. **Counterfactual dependency:** when does a source-bound historical lesson change the *goal itself*, not merely the reliability of a chosen feature?
2. **Discovery versus re-description:** when an agent discovers a “new” beneficiary from a rejected option, is that beneficiary already present in an older unindexed product text?
3. **The cost of memory:** could retrieving more relevant past reasoning crowd out genuinely independent interpretations, particularly when the same model authored the original possibilities?
4. **Missing sources:** how can an agent invite a valuable owner question without asserting that missing evidence proves a real human need?
5. **Generalization:** does a route to new goals seen in a learning product or game narrative generalize to other engineering domains, or do different domain theories dominate the creative leap?
6. **Contributory role:** what knowledge could only be reached because of retrospective source–relation links, and which conclusions would appear under equally careful source-only reading?
7. **Human value:** what kind of owner feedback would distinguish “surprising but irrelevant” from “an outcome I would not have considered and am glad to consider,” without treating adoption or implementation as mandatory?

**Bounded synthesis:** the promise of accumulated knowledge is **not storage volume** but its possible ability to surface a previously inaccessible, source-specific tension at the right moment; the *agent* then changes the **question of what users should be able to accomplish**. The possibility is coherent with ADRs 0031 and 0034, but none of the retained facts makes a novel user need logically necessary. A future agent's independent transformation and owner-confirmed relevance are the unproven causal steps. Sometimes memory merely helps avoid an error; sometimes ordinary reasoning can discover the same end; sometimes memory may anchor the imagination.
