## 1. Source/authority frame: what Atlas is, at this revision

Atlas's [canonical product thesis](https://github.com/ThorStarlord/atlas-midgard/blob/2b3556e47daa6110d01c045432e23bf4e041ba49/docs/vision/product-thesis.md) values **hardcore depth without hardcore stress**, persistent authored heroes/companions, attachment to inhabited places, optional cooperative play and *civilization that is already capable*. Its [narrative identity](https://github.com/ThorStarlord/atlas-midgard/blob/2b3556e47daa6110d01c045432e23bf4e041ba49/docs/vision/narrative-identity.md) moves through attachment, disturbance, responsibility, restoration and *remembered consequence*. The [player-experience identity](https://github.com/ThorStarlord/atlas-midgard/blob/2b3556e47daa6110d01c045432e23bf4e041ba49/docs/vision/player-experience-identity.md) values calm comprehension, reversible experimentation, legitimate low-attention play, and co-op that does **not** require learning a different game. The [Guardian arc](https://github.com/ThorStarlord/atlas-midgard/blob/2b3556e47daa6110d01c045432e23bf4e041ba49/docs/characters/guardian-protagonist-arc.md) already articulates mature heroism as **helping civilization carry itself**, not personally possessing every responsibility. These are *accepted ends*, not discoveries made here.

The [current README](https://github.com/ThorStarlord/atlas-midgard/blob/2b3556e47daa6110d01c045432e23bf4e041ba49/README.md) and [STATUS](https://github.com/ThorStarlord/atlas-midgard/blob/2b3556e47daa6110d01c045432e23bf4e041ba49/STATUS.md) describe three-Saga solo progression, Chronos, companion continuity, ordinary-life slices, Living Restoration and an **Epoch V construction focus on playable co-op integration**, not fully authored co-op ordinary-life social experiences. The implemented guest visible path at this revision is a bounded [Ruined Causeway host/guest localhost presentation](https://github.com/ThorStarlord/atlas-midgard/blob/2b3556e47daa6110d01c045432e23bf4e041ba49/docs/implementation/guest-replica-visibility.md), with [joined-guest Hearth rest](https://github.com/ThorStarlord/atlas-midgard/blob/2b3556e47daa6110d01c045432e23bf4e041ba49/docs/implementation/guest-hearth-rest.md); it is **not** evidence that guests can currently tour restored Larkspur scenes, be recognized as distinct visitors or enjoy the speculative experience below. The longer-term Living Restoration/Scale C destination is not a running co-op social system.

**Non-negotiable narrative boundary:** Atlas's [playable roster/party direction](https://github.com/ThorStarlord/atlas-midgard/blob/2b3556e47daa6110d01c045432e23bf4e041ba49/docs/decisions/playable-character-roster-and-control-model.md) and [host-authoritative co-op decision](https://github.com/ThorStarlord/atlas-midgard/blob/2b3556e47daa6110d01c045432e23bf4e041ba49/docs/systems/host-authoritative-coop-session.md) separate **actor identity from controller identity**. When an external human controls an existing Sigrid/Eira party seat, **Sigrid or Eira remains the fictional participant** for shared companion History. The remote controller does **not** become a new diegetic traveler simply by joining. Co-op players' profiles are different *playthrough biographies*, not canonical alternative universes or parallel timelines within one fiction. Any future experience must preserve that truth or explicitly request a separate design decision.

**Scope of novelty check:** reviewed Atlas's README, STATUS, product/player/narrative vision, Hearth and civilizational source documents, co-op and guest-reward authority, companion continuity, Larkspur hospitality/public memory, Living Continuity, Chronos and remembered-route implementation; inspected current co-op/runtime progression code and the reviewed `.sensemaking/knowledge/` records. Also cross-checked the existing Sensemaking creative archive. The absence of this *exact beneficiary goal* from the **inspected relevant corpus** supports only a **bounded NEW HYPOTHESIS**, not proof of global originality or market value.

It changed for you. I remember being there, though my own place still has its own unfinished history.”* Retain the two independent progression truths; use existing hospitality and public remembrance as **an interpretive model** for why witnessing/returning might feel worthwhile. Do **not** turn guest receipt into host canonical History, invent a persistent “other world” inside the cosmology, or imply a current networking feature.

The source-derived relationship is:

`host canonical Saga History → host-derived visible place`  
AND `guest's truthful participation receipt → guest's personal History, no home Saga state`  
PLUS `ordinary Hearth hospitality / public memory → potential emotional meaning of non-owning participation`  
→ **hypothetical** `player values helping and revisiting *another player's* distinct continuity`.

The first two arrows are documented system relations; **the last arrow is a creative player-value hypothesis**. There is currently no authored reviewed `mayEnable` edge connecting guest receipts to Hearth hospitality or visitor attachment. This relationship was synthesized by reading the source and knowledge records; no automatic graph inference is claimed.

## 3. Two perspectives, compared without calling it an experiment

### Architecture-first reading

Starting from [`owns_world_change=false`](https://github.com/ThorStarlord/atlas-midgard/blob/2b3556e47daa6110d01c045432e23bf4e041ba49/game/scripts/runtime/coop_participation_receipt.gd) and [History-derived route consequences](https://github.com/ThorStarlord/atlas-midgard/blob/2b3556e47daa6110d01c045432e23bf4e041ba49/game/scripts/progression/world_consequence_catalog.gd) yields a **structural asymmetry**: a single cooperative event can become a host's changed world and a guest's participation-only personal record. The [actor/controller boundary](https://github.com/ThorStarlord/atlas-midgard/blob/2b3556e47daa6110d01c045432e23bf4e041ba49/docs/systems/host-authoritative-coop-session.md) prevents simplistic “my avatar entered your world” fiction. The creative question becomes: *could the player enjoy caring for a lasting outcome they helped enable but do not own?*

### Outcome-first reading

Starting **separately as a reasoning lens**, from [the canonical attachment and generous-Guardian promise](https://github.com/ThorStarlord/atlas-midgard/blob/2b3556e47daa6110d01c045432e23bf4e041ba49/docs/vision/product-thesis.md), a plausible underserved experience is **peacefully visiting a friend's meaningful, historically changed places and being glad they flourish**, instead of only joining for rewards, combat or synchronized progress. This reading alone might invent a generic social visit, already adjacent to the accepted cozy/optional-co-op fantasy.

### Comparison and contribution

The architectural reading changes the outcome-oriented question: **the two players do not need identical history for their care to be mutual or meaningful**. The outcome lens challenges the architecture reading: a receipt and a technical invariant alone do not make a person feel welcomed, appreciated or attached; the *lived social and ordinary-life experience* would have to carry that meaning. The joint result is not “a better participation receipt”; it is the **hypothetical desired outcome of participating generously in a continuity one does not own**.

These are two passes **within one agent, after shared source access**. They are **not independent subagent runs**, controlled conditions, evidence that the knowledge graph caused novelty, or a demonstration that another capable agent would fail to imagine the same thing.

## 4. One concrete hypothetical player experience — “A place that is yours, and still matters to me”

**Status: FICTIONAL, FUTURE-CONDITIONAL PLAYER EXPERIENCE.** Not current gameplay footage, implemented plot, accepted feature or a canonical proposal.

**Initial situation.** Two friends have individual Atlas save profiles. One host has legitimately restored the Ruined Causeway and established a particular set of visible recovery markers. The other has not mastered the Causeway at home. They care about each other's characters and the warm ordinary life of Lantern Vale.

**Current documented ingredients, not the fictional result.** The host's [profile-derived WorldConsequenceCatalog](https://github.com/ThorStarlord/atlas-midgard/blob/2b3556e47daa6110d01c045432e23bf4e041ba49/game/scripts/progression/world_consequence_catalog.gd) can show restored markers in supported host route scenes; optional co-op provides a human-controlled existing companion seat in its bounded runtime; a guest present at a **new, committed host Saga completion** can later retain a truthful receipt **without home-world mastery**. No current source guarantees integrated guest visitation of the host's restored settlement, narrative guest acknowledgement, cross-profile commemorations or repeat-visit receipt awards.

**Hypothetical encounter.** Imagine a later integrated player experience, if independently desired and designed, in which the host can share a calm return to a restored Hearth/route with their friend. A warm civic place is visibly different because of the host's prior Saga History. The friends linger near the recut Causeway marker, watch ordinary residents maintain the road and talk about the crisis. Lantern Vale's road-gift custom and Many-Voiced Archive provide **a fictional motif** of welcoming and telling stories, **not** a claim that players can currently exchange gifts or record testimony.

A moment the guest might describe *outside the fiction*:

> “That road is safe in your story, not yet in mine. I didn't take your progress home. But I helped when it mattered, and coming back here with you still feels like returning to something we share.”

When the guest leaves, their own home save's Causeway need not be restored. If they were present when the host **actually newly completed** a supported Saga, their profile may hold the bounded participation History under existing authority. A **later social visit alone** would not automatically produce another receipt; the current identity rule is host+guest+Saga and admission alone yields nothing. The memory that motivates the fictional reunion is **player experience**, not a new asserted persistent save field.

**Actor/character truth constraint.** The guest may be **controlling Sigrid or Eira**, not acting as a separate visitor recognized by NPCs. The guest player's social memory and the character's diegetic biography are related at the session boundary but **not identical**. This hypothesis can exist at the player-to-player experiential layer. If later fiction were to portray a distinct visiting person, that would be a separate, unselected product/design change—not a consequence already delivered by co-op.

**Difference in the beneficiary's desired experience:** not “we both unlocked the same dungeon,” not “we both own the same restoration,” and not “my account gained a reward.” It is **a form of emotional belonging, generous heroism and attachment to another person's ongoing home**. A shared expedition's significance survives without synchronizing the participants' canonical worlds.

**Potential value:** the host could feel that their developed community is **worth visiting**, while the guest could care about and celebrate a friend's world without losing solo autonomy or needing matching progression. This fits Atlas's warmth + epic protection + restoration promise. Whether real players want this, and how to make it legible without constructing a social obligation, is **NOT_ASSESSED**.

## 5. Consequence structure: causal mechanisms and the ways they fail

| Level / mechanism | Plausible positive consequence | Harm or counterexample; where inference breaks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Direct: distinct history roles** | Host-owned canonical restoration can remain true while guest-owned `coop_participation` is also true. Their different **authority scopes** can become a player-relevant story of helping without owning. | Logical distinction is **source-established**; turning it into felt attachment is **hypothetical**. If players only want synchronized progression, it may feel unfair or empty. |
| **Direct: place as beneficiary** | A player could value seeing **another player's** familiar location transformed, not merely their own loot or checklist. | Integrated social visitation of restored Hearths is **not implemented** in the bounded guest Causeway scene; a source-derived marker is not a social experience by itself. |
| **Second-order: generous multiplayer motivation** | The reason to join could shift from “farm my next reward” toward “help my friend preserve something they love.” This could give optional co-op a distinct **nonextractive** emotional purpose. | Existing receipts grant Gold/Ore, and some players prioritize rewards. The product must not require gratitude performances or essential story access through co-op. |
| **Second-order: distinct persistent biographies** | The host and guest can remember different, truthful aspects of one session, potentially strengthening bonds between players while retaining individualized Saga mastery. | The guest receipt contains host ID, guest ID and Saga, **not** specific place-state changes or rich narrative witness testimony; imaginative specificity cannot be attributed to the current record. |
| **Systemic: attachment across invitations** | A visit could make one friend's restored Hearth feel socially meaningful without inventing a global shared universe, potentially opening a new layer of “places worth caring about.” | If visiting becomes an exclusive progression, prestige or emotional-completion requirement, the result conflicts with optional co-op and **attachment without FOMO**. |
| **Systemic: distributed heroism** | The game might offer a social analogue of the Guardian's source-established mature arc: **support capable others without taking their achievements as your own**. | This thematic alignment is already present in [the Guardian arc](https://github.com/ThorStarlord/atlas-midgard/blob/2b3556e47daa6110d01c045432e23bf4e041ba49/docs/characters/guardian-protagonist-arc.md). A story theme alone is **RESTATED**, not new mechanics or demonstrated player value. |
| **Unexpected: ownership asymmetry becomes an affordance** | The very restriction designed to stop guests overwriting host/own world could create a kind of **care without appropriation** rare in ordinary shared-progression framing. | Narrative credit is difficult: the host story attributes participation to the **diegetic companion**, not the human guest. Misrepresenting that would undermine Atlas's character identity. |
| **Unexpected: nonrepeatable receipt vs repeatable affection** | An already-restored world might still be **socially worth revisiting** even when no new participation receipt is eligible—suggesting the desired experience need not be another reward loop. | There is no evidence players will voluntarily revisit without rewards. No repeated-play effect, retention or user demand is claimed. |
| **Higher order: player-visible world difference** | Different legitimate profile histories could make ordinary-life places **meaningfully recognizable as another player's particular journey**, not simply a globally unlocked map. | Different profile states are not literal multiverses; if the presented world is visually/narratively indistinguishable, this outcome evaporates. If a host/guest presentation layer is absent, the player cannot experience the contrast. |

**Beneficial feedback, only if lived:** player values another's world → joining gains a meaning beyond transaction → their shared remembered crisis makes the host's place feel more personal → voluntary visits may enrich friendship and world attachment. No causal/behavioral evidence supports the loop yet.

**Harmful feedback, only if mishandled:** hosts feel pressure to provide impressive restoration states → guests feel like second-class labor or forced companion puppeteers → players begin comparing progression and seeking missing social content → “cozy” travel becomes FOMO or account-status hierarchy. Mere architecture **does not** settle the social psychology. Honoring separate owner/guest authority may increase **legitimacy** while still failing to create **emotional equality**.

**Surprise:** Atlas's strict truth separation yields **three identities**, not one: (1) the **host profile** whose world is canonical, (2) the **guest player profile** that may truthfully record participating without mastery and (3) the **diegetic named companion** whose fictional actions count as participation regardless of controller. The creative possibility arises from respecting all three. Most naive “shared adventure” proposals collapse at least two and would create false personal or narrative history.
