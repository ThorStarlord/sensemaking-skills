## Deliberate change of creative object

The first divergent pass mainly generated *new questions, capability combinations, future histories, interpretations and learning experiences*. This pass deliberately asks different kinds of questions:

- **Observability:** What would have to become witnessable for a future human to know what happened?
- **Meaning conservation:** What product promise could survive radically different representations, and what meaning would be lost?
- **Attention:** What would better use of knowledge look like if exposing *less* knowledge were sometimes desirable?
- **Situated validity:** Under which circumstances are apparently incompatible statements each appropriate?
- **Permission:** How could people explore together while controlling which knowledge may be shared or acted on?
- **Social practice:** What forms of collaboration, habit and institutional memory are produced *around* the code?

These are generative lenses, not a required ontology, process, evaluation criteria, or claim that the previous atlas was deficient. Candidate identities below are local to this dated record. Each distinguishes an imagined **user outcome** from the architectural technique one might someday use to realize it.

## Family K - Counterfactual observability

**Novel territory:** invent *conditions under which something could be known*, rather than generate another answer from existing evidence. Seeds: source-boundary and bounded-query-disclosure invariants, epistemic status, and the distinction between an observed trace and a user assertion.

### NX-K1 - Future-witness design
Imagine a repository owner asking: “In two years, could an agent explain *why a user abandoned this workflow* without inventing motive?” A future-witness workshop would work backward from questions of this kind and invent possible, ethically acquired forms of evidence or user testimony. Its output is a **landscape of newly answerable questions**, not a monitoring specification. This differs from **DE-A1 question archaeology**, which recovers motivating historical questions, and **DE-A3 unasked-question discovery**, which names missing inquiries but does not imagine what evidence could make an answer possible. Also belongs to **N** (conditions for valid claims) and **O** (permission for observation).

### NX-K2 - First-person evidence channels
Imagine developer tools recognizing that a person's confusion, relief or loss of trust cannot be reconstructed from commits and CI. A hypothetical product experience would invite an explicitly attributed, optional first-person account alongside software evidence, **without translating the account into a verified code fact**. This differs from **DE-H1 experiential concept discovery**, which proposes experiences as goals; here the creative object is how subjective experience may become a legitimately distinct knowledge source. Also belongs to **O** and **P**.

### NX-K3 - Parallel observation worlds
Imagine reviewing one repository through alternative, explicitly chosen modes of observation: user interruptions, handoff misunderstandings, or changing domain vocabulary. Each mode would make different questions answerable while leaving other phenomena invisible. The user explores the *consequences of what is observable*, without claiming one instrument reveals the whole system. This differs from **DE-G1 design disagreement laboratory** (multiple interpretations of a known problem): the variation here is in **what evidence could exist**, before anyone disagrees about it. Also belongs to **N**.

## Family L - Meaning-preserving transformation

**Novel territory:** treat product meaning as something that can be conserved, distorted or lost when software changes form. Seeds: the layered ontology's separation of Product, SoftwareSystem and Evidence; architecture contracts; domain concept distinctions; revision-bound source identity.

### NX-L1 - Promise-preservation studio
Imagine exploring several very different product forms while asking which original user promise must remain recognizable to its users. For example, a formerly file-oriented agent experience might become conversational, visual or event-based while preserving the user's ability to understand and challenge its claims. This is not **DE-J3 architectural optionality landscapes**, which explores unspecified futures an architecture permits; it is an exploration of **semantic continuity across otherwise dissimilar forms**. It also differs from **DE-C2 contract inversion**, which repurposes a producer-consumer relationship. Also belongs to **N**.

### NX-L2 - Semantic-loss cartography
Imagine a navigable account of meanings that **do not translate faithfully** when a product crosses a language, version, domain or interface boundary. Rather than treating non-equivalence as a defect to patch or a failed analogy, it could inspire experiences that deliberately reveal what cannot be carried over. This differs from **DE-I3 constructive analogy failure**, which invents possibilities from a nontransferable analogy, and from ordinary schema/contract reconciliation, which checks agreement with specified contracts. Also belongs to **N** and **P**.

### NX-L3 - Multi-era meaning lenses
Imagine an engineer switching between documented historical meanings of “responsibility,” “knowledge,” or “ready” within one repository, seeing which interpretation belongs to which time and authority context without forcing them into one permanent dictionary. This is not **DE-D1 alternative-history design worlds**: the lenses stay anchored to actual revision-specific meanings rather than imagining alternate histories. Nor is it **DE-B3 decision-language translation** for audiences: here *the underlying referent changed over time*. Also belongs to **N** and **M**.

## Family M - Cognitive ecology and purposeful forgetting

**Novel territory:** consider the burden, salience and life-cycle of what people must attend to, not merely the amount of knowledge retained. Seeds: bounded disclosure, source-pinned reconstruction, selective graph queries, the Grounding Pack's compact entry role, and the distinction between historic records and current authority.

### NX-M1 - Reconstructible oblivion
Imagine a developer-controlled experience that lets old distinctions fade from everyday view while retaining source pointers and the ability to reconstruct them on demand. A retired design might remain historically recoverable yet cease occupying the user's foreground. This differs from **poss:possibility-memory** and **DE-F3 repository museum**, both concerned with recovering remembered content; this asks what creative capacity becomes available when **not everything remembered is always presented**. No deletion, source suppression or actual retention policy is proposed. Also belongs to **N**.

### NX-M2 - Attention-shaped knowledge journeys
Imagine an agent presenting a tiny, expandable “what matters to this question” journey, where a person can choose to reveal assumptions, contradictions or evidence provenance one layer at a time. The subject of invention is *cognitive control over disclosure*, not a new explanation vocabulary. This differs from **DE-B3 decision-language translation** and the existing static context-pack capability: a context pack selects documents, while this hypothetical experience makes **the reader's changing attention** part of exploration. Also belongs to **O**.

### NX-M3 - Institutional myth ecology
Imagine tracking the difference between what a repository's community repeats as common wisdom and what its authoritative sources actually justify, then inventing experiences where those tensions can be explored constructively. For example, “tests prove the product works” may become a durable communal shortcut even though the repo explicitly separates mechanical PASS from product benefit. This differs from **DE-F3 repository museum** (historical intellectual display) and **DE-E2 counterexample apprenticeship** (teaching boundary recognition): this explores how *shared informal belief becomes a design material*. No claim is made that such a myth currently dominates a team. Also belongs to **P**.

## Family N - Situated validity and epistemic environments

**Novel territory:** explore knowledge as conditional on environment, perspective of observation and source availability, rather than simply true/false or fresh/stale. Seeds: exact-revision history, bounded knowledge queries, strategic continuity's currentness distinctions, and the separation of observed premises from hypotheses.

### NX-N1 - Applicability envelopes
Imagine a semantic navigator that lets a user ask, “Under *which system conditions* would this claim be informative?” A past architectural lesson could remain appropriate in one deployment shape and misleading in another, even if its citation is unaltered. This is not **poss:possibility-reconsideration-from-change**, which reopens candidates following relevant changes, or **DE-D2 unfulfilled-promise archaeology**, which searches gaps between intention and realization. Its object is the **domain of applicability**, not an event notification or unmet expectation. Also belongs to **L**.

### NX-N2 - Coexisting local truths
Imagine two seemingly contradictory engineering claims presented as **different conditional worlds**: each valid for an explicitly different target configuration, audience contract or historical scope. A creative agent might invent an experience for moving between these worlds without declaring a global winner. This differs from **DE-G1 creative disagreement**, where interpreters dispute one situation, and from the current contradiction query, which exposes conflicting records without necessarily modeling conditional coexistence. Also belongs to **K** and **L**.

### NX-N3 - Graceful semantic partiality
Imagine a deliberately incomplete knowledge environment becoming a coherent experience: an agent can show which new questions are still safely imaginable with a missing index, sparse domain coverage or inaccessible source, and which would require invented facts. This is not merely the existing **inv:bounded-query-disclosure** rule saying what a query omitted. It explores **positive forms of inquiry that remain possible under partial knowledge**. No new fallback runtime or verification result is claimed. Also belongs to **M** and **K**.

## Family O - Permission-shaped knowledge commons

**Novel territory:** explore knowledge as something that has readers, sources, consent and delegated uses without pretending that shared access grants shared decision authority. Seeds: the source-boundary invariant, ADR 0029 authority law, the active-agent semantic-controller constraint, and the [CME first-visit qualification](../normal-use/operational-qualification-001.md) distinguishing incomplete learner evidence and consent.

### NX-O1 - Knowledge escrow for imagination
Imagine contributors jointly exploring an unselected future where one person can offer an anonymized hypothesis or question without disclosing a confidential source. Others could discover compatible ideas, while source inspection and release remain separately authorized. This differs from **DE-B2 participatory knowledge creation**, which concerns joint construction and challenge of a model, and **DE-G3 collaborative possibility authorship**, which records plural contributors. Here the invention is a **conditional disclosure relationship**. Also belongs to **K**.

### NX-O2 - Personal epistemic overlays
Imagine developers temporarily arranging private “what I suspect / what I understand / what I need explained” views over the same public, source-grounded repository knowledge, without adding these views as authoritative graph claims. That could make exploratory disagreement comfortable without manufacturing competing truths. This differs from **DE-G3 plural creative authorship**: an overlay is an individual **learning and uncertainty interface**, not another version of the shared possibility. Also belongs to **M** and **P**.

### NX-O3 - Delegation-shaped possibility rooms
Imagine the same future explored in distinct rooms: one for free conceptual play, another for source-reviewed architectural discussion, a third for owner-authorized decisions. Crossing rooms would require an explicit act of authority rather than a powerful recommendation quietly becoming a task. This differs from **DE-G2 negotiable product futures**, which varies legitimate goals; the operative variation here is **what each participant may know, propose, endorse or perform**, even with the *same* goals. No permission engine, automated routing or workflow gate is proposed. Also belongs to **P**.

## Family P - Repository knowledge as social infrastructure

**Novel territory:** invent experiences from the practices, roles and cultural habits that software development generates around itself. The object is neither a feature inventory nor a history exhibit. Seeds: stewardship incidents and lessons, Campaign handoff/reconstruction, process/authority distinctions, and the product strategy's high-delegation user.

### NX-P1 - Ritual archaeology
Imagine recurring manual rituals—handoff recitations, release-check conversations, repeated explanations to newcomers—treated not just as toil to automate but as **social practices with hidden functions**. A future capability might retain the trust-building part while radically changing the mechanics. This differs from **DE-C3 dormant software affordances** (unexpected uses of existing interfaces) and **DE-E1 architectural apprenticeship** (learning from decisions): its input is the *practice among people*, not the implemented mechanism or lesson. Also belongs to **M**.

### NX-P2 - Participation horizons
Imagine exploring how one repository design changes **who is able to make a meaningful contribution later**: a new vocabulary, provenance view or boundary can change the range of participants without changing a feature's immediate function. This differs from **DE-B1 role migration**, which imagines the same capability for another beneficiary; here the object is **the distribution of future participation and agency** created by a design. Also belongs to **O** and **L**.

### NX-P3 - Institutional feedback worlds
Imagine a repository and its users co-evolving: a new explanation changes review habits; those habits change which evidence gets written; that evidence changes which future explanations can be generated. The creative output is a **possible feedback ecology**, not a planned loop or quantified prediction. This differs from **DE-D3 future-history sketching**, which narrates hypothetical eras of product identity, and **GD-F conceptual regime shift**, which infers a new concept from local changes. Here the generative object is a **reciprocal system of software and human practice**. Also belongs to **K** and **M**.

## Intersections, variations and unresolved paths

An idea can appear in more than one family: family labels are navigational, and intersections are **creative relationships**, not graph edges, prerequisites or inferred causal dependencies.

| Intersections | New question enabled by combining them |
| --- | --- |
| **NX-K1 + NX-O1** (K / O) | Could people design future evidence while retaining consent over whether that evidence may later be disclosed? |
| **NX-K2 + NX-P2** (K / O / P) | Could first-person accounts change who can participate in architecture reasoning without treating experience as a code fact? |
| **NX-L2 + NX-N2** (L / N / P) | Could a product make irreducibly different meanings legible instead of “repairing” them into false equivalence? |
| **NX-M1 + NX-N3** (M / N) | Could everyday semantic memory intentionally shrink while preserving an honest experience when a source is no longer in view? |
| **NX-M3 + NX-P1** (M / P) | Which informal engineering rituals transmit reliable knowledge, and which perpetuate unsupported lore? |
| **NX-O2 + NX-P2** (O / M / P) | Could personally attributed uncertainty make contribution more accessible without giving private interpretations shared authority? |
| **NX-K3 + NX-P3** (K / N / P) | Could the *choice of what people observe* reshape future collaboration and in turn the evidence that gets retained? |

### Variations not collapsed into one design

- **Witness type:** code/runtime trace; owner intent; attributed first-person report; missing or deliberately uncollected evidence.
- **Meaning transition:** revision over time; migration between domains; translation between representations; partial non-equivalence.
- **Memory exposure:** archive everything; selective foreground; reversible hiding; source-reconstruction on request.
- **Applicability:** time alone; deployment configuration; user/organizational practice; consent state; incomplete knowledge.
- **Participation:** solitary developer; returning agent; multi-role team; end user as a knowledge source; participant with limited disclosure rights.
- **Social feedback:** software changes people; people change documentation; documentation changes software; all three mutually affect what can be observed.

These are unexamined axes to revisit, not quality criteria, a completeness matrix or suggested execution sequence.

### Explicitly unexamined directions

This pass does **not** investigate physical-environment context, energy/ecological externalities of software, embodied or non-textual modes of engineering knowledge, machine-to-machine epistemic negotiation, or non-software communities as primary users. Those remain open dimensions, neither rejected nor established product opportunities. Likewise, proposals that would require changing accepted authority or retention boundaries are thought experiments only; their speculative status does not weaken existing constraints.

## Novelty and evidence accounting

All NX entries are **provisionally NEW relative to the bounded inspected landscape**, not globally original inventions or newly qualified product capabilities. Each entry names its closest earlier neighbor and specifies a distinct creative object: observable evidence, meaning conservation, attention, conditional applicability, permission relations or social feedback. If later review finds a genuinely identical precedent, mark it RESTATED or REVISED in a *later* dated comparison instead of rewriting this originating record. Related overlap is a feature, not automatic duplication.

The existing [Idea Library](../idea-library.md) retains its 19 curated records unchanged. This pass makes **no** new poss:, arch:, conseq:, ontology, graph, Skill, validation, Strategic Frontier, issue, roadmap or work-item entry. Historical source facts were reviewed; the imagined experiences were not prototyped, tested, adopted, independently discovered by a returning agent, or shown to improve decisions. The valid stopping point is the expanded, navigable **possibility space alone**.
