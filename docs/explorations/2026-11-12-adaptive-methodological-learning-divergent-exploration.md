## 1. Why another inquiry is a different question

The source synthesis already reconciles the existence and scope of methodological learning guidance. The [prior adaptive-metareasoning Stage 0–1 inquiry](2026-11-07-adaptive-metareasoning-divergent-exploration.md) opened M01–M18 about choosing/correcting a method or question, and its later stages reached a bounded research stop. Those historical outcomes remain intact.

This new owner-requested question is **not** "Which new automatic metareasoning controller should Sensemaking build?" Nor is it "Does the repository lack a MethodLesson ontology type?" Both presume a gap not established by the source.

Instead:

### Q1 — Decision-time encounter
What could make relevant methodological experience *available for a later consequential choice* without confusing storage, retrieval, exposure, use, and benefit?

### Q2 — Defeasible transfer
How might an agent learn *when not to imitate* a precedent across sessions, models, hosts, tasks, and repositories while still obtaining value from it?

### Q3 — What is the unit of learning?
Should the learned object be a preference among `ACT / INQUIRE / CHALLENGE / EXPLORE / VERIFY / ESCALATE / STOP`, a relation among methods and questions, a way to discover previously unasked questions, or a conditional account of why no special method is needed?

### Q4 — Judgment and value
What observation could distinguish beneficial methodological adaptation from post-hoc rationalization, changed evidence exposure, or activity that merely looks reflective?

### Q5 — Whose experience matters?
Could methodological learning serve not only the coding agent but also a human maintainer, a later independent agent, or a recipient repository with different authority and outcomes?

Q1–Q5 overlap. They do not form a mandatory sequence, a list of product requirements, or a fixed evaluation protocol.

### Frames

- **F1 — Same-session agent:** beneficiary is the active coding agent; desired end is a better next bounded decision at acceptable reasoning cost. Its evidence may still be present in context.
- **F2 — Fresh successor:** beneficiary is a later agent with no private continuity; desired end is correct reconstruction and context-qualified use *or rejection* of an earlier method lesson.
- **F3 — Receiving repository:** a different repository receives an external precedent; its current goals, source state, constraints and authority govern. A transferred lesson carries no original repository decision power.
- **F4 — Maintainer:** beneficiary is a human assessing what helped or hindered a consequential choice without requiring private chain-of-thought, artificial metrics or a mandatory audit ritual.
- **F5 — Counterfactual non-learning:** the desired end may be independence, brevity or freedom from stale methodological lore; retention is not presumed beneficial.

### Lenses actually used

Temporal exposure (before/after choice), mechanism versus surface resemblance, boundary inversion, adverse outcomes, question/beneficiary change, epistemic observability, method-produced evidence, governance, and deliberate non-retention. Naming a lens is not proof that it created a novel idea.

## 3. Bounded possibility landscape

The local `C` labels are **CandidateExpressions within this document**, not `poss:` identities, code requirements, acceptance criteria, or counts toward earlier DE/M portfolios. Families overlap. Each expression states its proposed change, meaningful distinction and counterpressure; the examples are hypothetical unless explicitly sourced.

### Family A — Learning that actually reaches a choice

#### C1 — Decision-window rendezvous

**Expression:** A later agent might encounter a source-pinned methodological precedent *at the moment when it could alter a consequential method or responsibility choice*, rather than beginning every task by loading a growing book of lessons. Encounter could be a contextual recall through existing knowledge and reference surfaces; this is a desired decision experience, not a proposed background recommender.

**Difference:** The unit of success changes from "a lesson was saved" to "the right kind of knowledge was available before a particular decision." This refines the source synthesis's explicit evidence ceiling and M13's retention-oriented context indexing.

**Counterpressure/unknown:** Opportunistic recall can bias the agent before it understands present sources; an absent encounter might be the correct cheap behavior. No current evidence proves an agent will perform this rendezvous.

#### C2 — Justified non-imitation

**Expression:** An agent encountering a relevant historical recommendation may explicitly reject its prescribed activity because the receiving task differs in a consequential way, while retaining the precedent as useful knowledge. In a simple reversible repair, `ACT` may be justified even when an earlier high-risk project benefited from `CHALLENGE`.

**Difference:** Benefit is not counted by how often prior methods are copied. A *contextually defensible refusal* may be a successful use of memory, even if behavior matches an agent that never saw the lesson.

**Counterpressure/unknown:** A retrospective refusal could be invented to appear reflective. Distinguishing principled rejection from ignored evidence requires a bounded, pre-choice account or independent observation, not invented private reasoning.

#### C3 — The unseen-but-available lesson

**Expression:** A team could distinguish a known precedent that was in a source index but not surfaced to the agent from one that was surfaced but judged inapplicable. These produce different learning problems even when the resulting engineering decision looks identical.

**Difference:** This is a *delivery-versus-applicability* question at the decision boundary, rather than a claim that the underlying reasoning method is defective. It overlaps M08's method-delivery failure but specifically concerns the retained lesson's temporal encounter.

**Counterpressure/unknown:** Non-retrieval is not necessarily a failure; proving a missed encounter requires knowledge of the relevant source and actual accessible context. There is no mandate to log all absent documents.

### Family B — Transfer without importing authority

#### C4 — Mechanism-first precedent comparison

**Expression:** A receiving agent might assess precedent by the *mechanism of the problem*—for instance, premature closure despite an incomplete task-target comparison—instead of by similar repository names, tools or labels. The same visible symptom may have different causes.

**Difference:** Transfer becomes a conditional analogy about explanatory structure and counterexamples, not a similarity score. It deepens the existing context-qualified transfer guidance rather than replacing it.

**Counterpressure/unknown:** Mechanism attribution may be uncertain; superficial differences sometimes matter because they signal different host affordances or authority contracts. A guessed common cause cannot authorize copying an approach.

#### C5 — Boundary-flip interrogation

**Expression:** Instead of asking only "Where else could this method help?", ask for a minimal change of condition that would make it harmful, pointless or unauthorized. An earlier valuable evidence-first inquiry could become delay for a later cheap, reversible implementation.

**Difference:** An applicability limit becomes productive knowledge, not a footnote after a universalized rule. It proposes a way of examining *exceptions* rather than accumulating endorsements.

**Counterpressure/unknown:** Imagined boundary flips are hypotheses, not observed failures. This examination should remain proportional and need not activate for ordinary clear tasks.

#### C6 — Feedback from refused transfer

**Expression:** A recipient repository's attributed refusal of a lesson could inform the lesson's future scope: not "the source was wrong", but "under conditions X the proposed generalization does not travel." The source episode remains intact; later interpretations may narrow its claim.

**Difference:** C2 concerns an agent's immediate decision. C6 concerns *longitudinal feedback* to a reusable lesson after a distinct receiving episode. No source repository gains authority over the recipient.

**Counterpressure/unknown:** One refusal may reflect misunderstanding, contrary evidence or different goals; none establishes that the original lesson should be retired globally. Reconciliation and review remain separately required.

### Family C — Beyond preferring a single control move

#### C7 — Learning conditional compositions

**Expression:** Experience might teach when two reasoning activities help each other, compete, or are unnecessary in combination. A small `INQUIRE` can make a later `ACT` safer; `CHALLENGE` may be redundant when direct `VERIFY` can settle the same claim. What transfers is a conditional relationship, not a fixed sequence or score.

**Difference:** Extends M17's intellectual-work composition from diagnosis within one episode to learning a scoped *relationship between operations* across episodes.

**Counterpressure/unknown:** It could turn into a disguised mandatory workflow. A case that benefited from an ordering does not show that all future cases should reproduce it.

#### C8 — Learning to discover better questions

**Expression:** The transferable learning might be a *way of noticing unasked questions*, not a preference for one of the seven named control moves. A prior method may have revealed that technically correct answers missed affected users or future product ends; a later agent could use that as a tentative lens while preserving the new repository's distinct purpose.

**Difference:** M04/M18 address relationship-driven question discovery and productive question evolution **within** an inquiry. C8 asks how this generative capacity might carry between inquiries without transferring the original questions as obligations.

**Counterpressure/unknown:** A familiar reframing technique can produce fashionable but irrelevant questions. A valuable prior question is not evidence that the receiving repository has the same goal.

#### C9 — Methods alter what future evidence becomes visible

**Expression:** The chosen intellectual activity may shape the *observations* on which future method lessons are based. `VERIFY` finds closure errors that unchecked `ACT` might never surface; `EXPLORE` generates alternatives that routine execution would not record. An apparent record of "what works" is partially produced by what agents chose to look for.

**Difference:** This is an **endogenous evidence** question: the learning process changes its own observation conditions. It is not the same as M09's distinction among kinds of progress or a simple mode-selection failure.

**Counterpressure/unknown:** An attractive causal story about missing outcomes is not evidence of them. Any later comparison must account for different exposure and observation windows, not assume unobserved defects existed.

### Family D — Knowing whether methodological learning helped

#### C10 — Temporal evidence ladder for influence

**Expression:** Assess separately whether a lesson was retained, accessible, encountered *before* the choice, judged applicable or inapplicable, reflected in an observable decision, and connected to an outcome. Recording concise, source-linked decisions could help when consequential; no hidden chain-of-thought or universal telemetry is needed.

**Difference:** The synthesis already states the distinctions; this expression shifts the object of inquiry to the **time-sensitive transition** where influence could plausibly occur.

**Counterpressure/unknown:** A pre-choice note may still be incomplete or strategically postured; merely authoring a note does not prove actual cognitive causation. More instrumentation may cost more than the decision warrants.

#### C11 — A comparison that can disappoint

**Expression:** If a consequential learning claim requires comparative support, consider a limited like-for-like observation of an independent later agent *with and without* access to a retained, applicable method lesson. Ask whether task comprehension, chosen inquiry, product-value consequences, and authority adherence improve or worsen—not whether the agent merely repeats the lesson's phrasing.

**Difference:** Distinguishes qualitative *benefit* from storage, compliance and method-output resemblance. This is a hypothetical evidence path, not an experiment request or a claim of completed qualification.

**Counterpressure/unknown:** Agent competence, host, accessible sources, task equivalence, exposure sequence and review judgments confound comparisons; cost may be unjustified for low-consequence changes. A negative result must remain publishable.

#### C12 — Delayed value and unintended harm

**Expression:** A method could appear superior during one session yet lead to more downstream rework, narrower product imagination, or overconfident closure. Conversely, a slow inquiry could produce valuable future avoidance of a costly mistake. Learning could be assessed against *longer-horizon repository and beneficiary outcomes*, not immediate activity volume.

**Difference:** Changes the success horizon rather than adding another method category or test count.

**Counterpressure/unknown:** Attribution decays over time and other interventions accumulate; long observation costs money and can postpone useful construction. Later success cannot automatically be credited to earlier reasoning.

### Family E — Multiple interpreters and governed learning

#### C13 — A plurality of valid, conflicting precedents

**Expression:** Two source-grounded lessons might legitimately recommend different methods because their stakes, reversibility or goals differ. A receiving agent could preserve the contrast and the conditions of each, rather than collapsing them into one global "best practice."

**Difference:** The desirable outcome is *recoverable disagreement with bounded applicability*, not a winner, composite score or new automatic arbitration engine.

**Counterpressure/unknown:** A large unstructured set of precedents can overwhelm decision making and invite cherry-picking. Divergence is useful only where the present choice actually depends on the contrasting conditions.

#### C14 — Authority quarantine on travel

**Expression:** A methodological lesson could travel as information while its source project's authority does not. The receiving agent may use the experience to ask better questions, but current product strategy, source state, repository rules and protected decisions still govern.

**Difference:** Makes **authority survival versus semantic reuse** the central object, not merely correct lexical translation. This largely restates the synthesis's fourth transfer boundary; its role is a protective counterfactual within the landscape, not a newly discovered ontology gap.

**Counterpressure/unknown:** Too much provenance ceremony can suppress harmless reuse. No trans-repository policy is authorized by this expression.

#### C15 — Human-inspectable methodological disagreement

**Expression:** A maintainer could ask, "What source-backed reason made exploration worthwhile here but not in the earlier case?" and receive a reconstruction of contextual conditions, conflicting precedents, alternative interpretations and evidence limits without a purported transcript of the model's hidden thoughts.

**Difference:** Changes the **beneficiary and product experience** from adaptive agent control to human understanding of conditional engineering judgment. It may be achieved with existing documents and queries; no UI or new schema follows automatically.

**Counterpressure/unknown:** A plausible narrative can hide hindsight bias, and asking humans to audit every decision would damage usability. Real maintainer need has not been independently observed here.

### Family F — Learning less can also be learning

#### C16 — Selective silence instead of a larger canon

**Expression:** Methodological memory might improve agent performance by choosing which stale, marginal, contradictory or high-friction advice **not** to foreground, while preserving original evidence for reconstruction. The success condition could be reduced distraction without lost important exceptions.

**Difference:** Challenges the premise that more retained methodological material should always influence more decisions. This relates to the earlier atlas's purposeful-forgetting theme but targets **method guidance at the moment of choice**, not deletion or universal retrieval-ranking.

**Counterpressure/unknown:** Silence can conceal essential cautions and make learning harder to audit. No automatic suppression rule is justified without evidence and recoverability.

#### C17 — Fresh-first comparison as an anti-anchoring alternative

**Expression:** For some open-ended inquiries, a later agent might first form a bounded view of the current repository *without* being exposed to a prior methodology summary, then contrast it with the historical lesson. The result could be a new interpretation, a warranted agreement, or a discovery that the precedent is irrelevant.

**Difference:** Extends the [inquiry method reflection](2026-11-02-inquiry-method-reflection.md)'s independent pre-recall lens to **methodological inheritance** rather than only creative candidate generation. It is an explicitly known variant, not a globally novel proposal.

**Counterpressure/unknown:** Independent reconstruction can waste scarce context and repeat solved mistakes; pre-recall independence is not inherently better than timely guidance. It is not a mandatory two-pass gate.

#### C18 — No reusable methodological change

**Expression:** The best result of several normal-use episodes might be to preserve their observations and **decline** extracting reusable doctrine. Differences in goal, host, evidence or consequences might make a local explanation more truthful and useful than a universal rule. Successful learning can be a warranted `NO_CHANGE` or `STOP`.

**Difference:** Rejects the assumption that "adaptive methodological learning" must eventually produce a learning engine, new Skill, new schema or method preference. This is a counterfactual future emphasizing restraint and preservation of local competence.

**Counterpressure/unknown:** Excessive restraint could perpetuate rediscovery and obscure a genuinely recurring failure. The balance must follow concrete episodes, not a categorical preference for either documentation or change.

## 5. Illustrative contrasts that could falsify premature equivalence

These are thought experiments; neither agent behavior nor outcome was observed in this pass.

1. **Helpful refusal versus irrelevant storage.** Agent A reaches a trivial, reversible source fix and selects `ACT` despite having read a historical "challenge high-consequence claims" lesson; the rejection is justified by different stakes. Agent B also selects `ACT` but never encounters the lesson. Same action, different potential learning claim: C2 versus C3/C10. The correct fix does not distinguish them.
2. **Transfer of mechanism versus vocabulary.** Two repositories report "work done" with a missing user-relevant outcome. In one, a component-only inspection substituted for repository-level responsibility selection; in the other, a genuinely sufficient component repair completed the delegated scope. Matching the phrase "premature closure" does not prove the mechanism or the warranted control move is the same. C4/C5/C14 make that difference explicit.
3. **Learning from selectively observed failures.** A method that always verifies closure will record more detected closure mistakes than a method that seldom verifies. This alone does not show verification caused more defects. C9/C11/C12 require considering selection, detection and downstream consequences before claiming method quality.
4. **New reasoning benefit versus new process tax.** A repository with repeated, consequential retrieval failures might benefit from encounter-aware method lessons. A mature, well-grounded one-line change may benefit from no visible metareasoning at all. C1/C16/C18 represent both possibilities without treating either as the default future.

## 6. Open territory, limitations and stopping boundary

**Potentially consequential unknowns:**

- Which actual later-agent decision will reveal, from an accessible primary record, whether a methodological lesson was encountered before selection? No such case was exercised here.
- When does a rejection show better judgment, and when is it merely a polished after-the-fact narrative?
- Can recipient-repository feedback refine the scope of a lesson without making the source repository a governance authority?
- What observations distinguish better question discovery from familiar reframing language?
- How much of method-related value is visible immediately versus only through later product consequences?
- Could selectively **not** surfacing method history preserve creative independence, and how could that be evaluated without breaking legitimate continuity?
- Is a maintainer-facing account actually needed, or do current source/PR/Campaign surfaces already explain enough?
- How might evidence from different models, execution hosts and repo types expose conditional rather than universal method effects?

**What was not assessed:** full repository-wide duplicate search, external research and field literature, actual host/tool exposure traces, fresh-agent transfer, measured outcome benefits, new user demand, exhaustive code consumers, and the merits of a new machine schema. An uninspected surface is `NOT_ASSESSED`, not absent. No corpus search proves global novelty.

**Stop:** This bounded divergence has exposed several materially different *questions and beneficiaries* beyond the method-choice mechanics already explored—especially encounter timing, warranted non-imitation, method-conditioned evidence, human inspectability and legitimate non-learning. It also explicitly retained known restatements and counter-frames rather than presenting all expressions as discoveries. Further enumeration of cosmetic implementations would add little to this inquiry. **End here with a landscape**, not a ranking or a transition to Coverage Assessment, Convergent Evaluation, Depth Exploration, implementation or qualification.
