## Family A - Inventing questions

**Source inspiration:** existing architectural decisions and the Strategic Exploration Funnel, whose inquiries begin with intended outcomes and unexamined product dimensions.

| Candidate | What becomes possible / distinctive mechanism | Different from |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **DE-A1. Question archaeology** | Reconstruct the motivating problem behind a past design and discover newly relevant versions of that original question. | `why`/decision history reconstructs rationale; this generates new *questions* from that rationale. |
| **DE-A2. Question collision** | Combine two previously independent unresolved questions to uncover a third, unanticipated problem or user opportunity. | `poss:assumption-aware-opportunity-ecology` relates futures; this recombines inquiries before futures are defined. |
| **DE-A3. Unasked-question discovery** | Expose neglected problem dimensions such as delight, comprehensibility or confidence that conventional implementation discussions never asked about. | Knowledge-coverage inspection finds absent records; here the absence is a *kind of inquiry*. |

## Family I - Transfer without copying

**Source inspiration:** `poss:cross-product-transfer`, explicit target boundaries and source-verification rules. None of these ideas implies authorization to expand to or mutate another repository.

| Candidate | What becomes possible / distinctive mechanism | Different from |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **DE-I1. Cross-domain mechanism translation** | Use the product meaning of one repository's mechanism to imagine an unrelated experience elsewhere without importing source claims as facts. | Conventional transfer copies a known pattern; here analogy invents *a different product outcome*. |
| **DE-I2. Product-pattern fission** | Break a known pattern into essential relations, constraints and outcomes, then recombine them into new patterns. | Pattern reuse preserves the pattern; fission deliberately *dissolves its familiar form*. |

## Open possibilities for separate future divergence

- Can agents invent *unasked questions* better than they can propose features?
- Which user experiences are obscured by implementation-centered terminology?
- How might architectural knowledge become a medium for teaching design judgment?
- Could false starts and failed analogies be more generative than successful transfers?
- What entirely different product does the same capability suggest when its beneficiary changes?
- Which speculative perspectives remain invisible because the historical Idea Library anchors exploration?
- Can multiple valid concept maps coexist without inventing multiple semantic authorities?
