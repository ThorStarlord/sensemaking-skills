## Classification

### One Skill vs. multiple Skills (Option C vs. Option A)
**KEEP.** Nothing in six stress cases exercised the packaging boundary itself — every failure/ambiguity found (Cases 3, 4, 5) was about field semantics and rule wording, not about needing a second Skill file to fix. This round adds no new evidence either direction on Option A vs. C specifically; the packaging question remains exactly where the second-pass report left it (a behavioral rule surviving isolation is not evidence for a specific packaging choice) — but nothing this round found demands revisiting it.

### Diagnose/Interact responsibility separation
**KEEP.** Interact correctly self-scoped in every case it ran in (Cases 1, 2, 3): it never attempted to re-diagnose, never confused its role with Diagnose's, correctly deferred to "re-run Diagnose with a narrower focus" as an instruction rather than doing it itself. No case produced friction at this specific boundary.

### Durable brief as boundary
**KEEP, strengthened.** Case 6 is the strongest test of this claim in the engagement's history (thinnest brief yet) and held cleanly. Case 5 additionally confirmed the boundary holds even when the brief itself contains an internal, undisclosed-until-now-visible conflict — the consumer worked entirely from the document, surfaced the conflict rather than needing to re-diagnose to find it.

### Section 15 overall
**KEEP, one required revision identified.** The mechanism (production-real skeleton/reconcile wiring, non-blocking validation) held up under adversarial synthetic input (Case 5) exactly as designed. But Boundary Rule 6 — the one place a downstream consumer is instructed how to use Section 15 — has a real, demonstrated textual ambiguity (Case 5) that produces different verdicts on identical input depending on which reading an agent picks. This needs a wording fix before further reliance, though the underlying mechanism does not.

### Each Section 15 field individually

- **`uncertainty.source` / `.question`**: **KEEP, one REVISE noted.** Still the most load-bearing field (drove the entire branch structure in Cases 1-3). The REVISE is narrow and specific: the `repository_evidence`/`empirical` boundary needs sharper definition for "has X already happened" operational-history questions (Case 3's finding) — the field and its top-level branching logic are sound; one sub-boundary within it isn't.
- **`consequential_boundary` / `is_demonstrated_weakness`**: **KEEP, REVISE required.** Real, repeated, verdict-changing effect (Case 4's actual mechanism, Case 5's entire finding) — this is doing real work, more than `discovery_confidence` ever demonstrated. But Case 5 shows Boundary Rule 6's text needs to state explicitly whether this field's weakness must relate to Section 6's weakest boundary to count toward partial-coverage scoring.
- **`domain`**: **UNKNOWN this round.** No case exercised it (Cases 1/3/6 had no Section 15 or no `domain`; Cases 2/4/5 didn't populate it or didn't need to). Carries forward the first-pass finding (one real disclosure effect) unchanged, neither strengthened nor weakened here.
- **`discovery_confidence`**: **REVISE or DROP — the clearest negative finding this round produced.** Case 4 didn't just fail to observe a `low`-value effect (the prior "untested" framing) — it positively confirmed no consumer instruction anywhere currently reads this field at all, in either of the two places Section 15 is used. Either wire it into Boundary Rule 6 and Interact's decision tree explicitly, or drop it. Keeping it as-is (present, filled in, read by nothing) is the exact "field exists → no behavior changes → no consumer cares" pattern the owner named as the drop criterion before this round started.
- **`owner_intent_state` (`known`/`status`)**: **KEEP.** `status: thin` correctly gated Case 2's investigation without over-triggering a hard stop; `known` was correctly treated as background rather than a resolving answer in every case that had it. No new finding, but no erosion either.

### Downstream reliance on the brief
**KEEP, strengthened.** `architectural-review` performed correctly across the widest range of input quality tested yet — from a real, well-formed brief (real-runtime run) through a genuinely thin one (Case 6) through a deliberately self-contradictory one (Case 5) — without fabricating, without silently resolving ambiguity, and while producing genuinely specific risk analysis in every case. This is the single most consistently strong result across the whole round.

### Is any new architecture actually demanded by observed failures?
**No.** Every finding this round is a wording/documentation-level fix (Boundary Rule 6's scoping clause, `uncertainty.source`'s repository_evidence/empirical sub-boundary, `discovery_confidence`'s wiring-or-removal) or a field-level KEEP/DROP call, not a structural redesign. Nothing observed argues for a different packaging, a new Skill, a new validator, or a new governance mechanism. This itself is a real, positive result: the architecture's *shape* survived six adversarial cases; only some of its *field-level wording and one rule's scoping* needs revision.
