## 2. S1 — Genuine convergence without selected direction

**Source:** H1, introduction and §§1, 5–6.

**Original task/question:** Which substantially different *user/product outcomes* remain meaningfully distinct after reconciling overlapping exploratory expressions? Its source inventory preserves GD, DE, NX, RP and curated `poss:` records.

**ConsideredChoiceSet C1:** Several meaningful possible outcomes; H1 also identifies unresolved comparative boundaries and directions worth separate depth investigation.

**Judgment J1:** The report provides an evaluated landscape with qualified candidates for possible future depth. It explicitly says it does **not** select a product direction, architecture, strategic frontier, or implementation task. The asserted sufficiency is for **considering** further inquiry, not adopting one alternative.

**Selection status:** **no ScopedDirectionSelection in this historical artifact**. This is *not* proof that no later decision ever occurred; it states what H1 itself did.

**Alternatives:** remain source-identifiable; an unselected direction is not globally rejected or inferior. No count of 59 local labels proves independent distinctness or adequate strategic depth.

**Negative case:** A future agent reading H1's phrase "directly thesis-relevant outcomes" must not infer `Decision selects` or owner ratification.

## 3. S2 — Preferred inquiry is a recommendation, not adoption

**Source:** H2, §§6–8.

**ChoiceQuestion Q1:** Which *specific residual intellectual inquiry* deserves the next attention under the current thesis and known evidence?

**Candidate and approach:** H2 prefers asking how a high-delegation owner could recognize an omitted end, challenge a beneficiary assumption, or revise the question while keeping delegation useful. H2 separately retains question inheritance, future-witness observability, plural interpretation, architectural generativity, and a causal-benefit inquiry whose relative value depends on independent evidence access.

**ConvergenceRationale J2:** Under H2's current frame, owner-agency inquiry has high expected value in clarifying whether the owner can meaningfully redirect an agent-authored possibility landscape. The report also identifies alternatives and reversal conditions: changed owner purpose/inheritance failure, newly available evidence, a different source relationship or new beneficiary frame.

**CandidateWarrant:** a *reason to propose* a new bounded depth inquiry exists. H2 is not a record of actually performing that inquiry, independently measuring beneficiary understanding, or receiving owner adoption.

**Selection status:** **recommended**. No source in H2 by itself proves an actual owner choice, `ScopedDirectionSelection` or authority to run a protected downstream operation.

**Comparison level warning:** H2's broader strategic synthesis of Futures + Continuity as source-grounded prospective reasoning whose judgment improves across sessions is a product-direction **interpretation**, not a newly ratified thesis or selected architecture. It is not the same target as the recommended owner-agency inquiry.

## 5. S4 — Architecture preference, feedback and selection are separable

**Source:** P1, §§2, 6–8. **Type:** existing **ontology/contract example**, not a live newly selected architecture.

**ChoiceQuestion Q3:** Within a specified architecture/path comparison, which option appears preferable under the current assumptions and consequences?

**ProvisionalPreference:** a comparison may favor an `ArchitectureOption` or `ConstructionPath`. Post-convergence consequence exploration can `REAFFIRM`, `REVISE_PREFERENCE`, `REOPEN_ALTERNATIVES`, `INVESTIGATE`, `DEFER`, `NO_CHANGE`, or require owner/thesis attention.

**Scope:** P1 explicitly distinguishes a preferred architecture from a selected direction and a selected direction from implementation authorization. Existing `evaluates`, `prefers`, `selects` endpoints are architecture/path-specific.

**Negative case:** A future agent must not label P1's abstract feedback outcomes as a historical owner selection, or broaden its executable endpoints to `InquiryQuestion` or `ProductPossibility`.

## 6. S5 — Legitimate nonselection and inadequate option set

**Sources:** W1 §5–6 and O1 procedure. **Type:** explicitly **normative/illustrative**, not a reported historical decision.

**W1a — no selection despite relative preference (illustration):** Option A could appear preferable to option B under a declared criterion. Yet A depends on an unsupported premise and B would violate current authority. The agent may record the comparative conclusion but should report `NO_SELECTION`, `MORE_EVIDENCE_REQUIRED`, or `AUTHORITY_REQUIRED` as appropriate, not invent a selected target.

**O1 — option set incomplete:** a packet comparing two related downstream construction paths may omit an evidence-grounded upstream alternative. The owner-decision-capsule rule requires a return of `OPTION_SET_INCOMPLETE`, explaining the missing trajectory rather than manufacturing a binary owner choice.

**Decision semantics:** an unresolved finding can legitimately stop a bounded inquiry. Source-access, feasibility and owner preference must not be invented to make the record feel complete.

## 7. S6 — A proposed synthesis is not a selected new goal

**Source:** H2 §7 with H1 §6. **Type:** historical *interpretation* plus explicitly hypothetical selection counterexample.

**Interpretation:** H2 suggests that Futures and Continuity can combine into a strong current-thesis proposition, with Truth, Structure, Experience and Plurality playing supporting or complementary roles. H2 expressly states this is **not selection of a new architecture or proof the combination works**.

**Hypothetical next decision:** An agent could formulate a distinct synthesis if it names a concrete owner-valued end, explains how constituents interact, identifies possible adverse consequences and evidence needs, and specifies the authority needed for adoption.

**Invalid shortcut:** "All six directions are valuable, so select all six." This neither identifies a distinct combined product outcome nor supplies comparative warrant or a selecting actor.

**Reconsideration boundary R1:** a changed owner goal, independent owner-value evidence, previously hidden tradeoff or newly available source could alter the synthesis's relative attractiveness. That trigger does not retroactively change the historical H2 judgment or automatically authorize implementation.
