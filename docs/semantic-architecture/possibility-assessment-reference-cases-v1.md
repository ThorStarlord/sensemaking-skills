## 2. Case C1 — Bounded sufficiency is not global saturation

**Producing activity:** A (coverage), later reconsidered by N (combined coverage/portfolio).
**Assessed targets:** three divergent passes and accompanying GD family material in the inspected corpus.
**F1 — Assessment frame:** current Sensemaking product thesis; question is whether the available conceptual variety is **sufficient to consider** a *separate* Convergent Evaluation. This is not a claim that every worthwhile idea has been found.

**J1 — Historical CoverageFinding:** A section 8 provisionally answers yes. Its sections 4–7 identify strong method overlaps, newer territory and important unanswered perspectives. N sections 2–3 judges the landscape sufficiently varied for a *provisional* inquiry portfolio but notes shallow longitudinal question inheritance, independent question formation, owner judgment, public/ecological effects and real beneficiary evidence.

| Component of coverage | Recorded interpretation | What cannot follow |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Corpus scope | Explicitly inspected archive passes in A; larger inspected source set for N | That the world's full possibility space was inspected |
| Conceptual breadth | Materially different outcome families and perspectives appear | Each candidate label is a separate product opportunity |
| Depth | Several consequence questions remain shallow despite broad ideas | Breadth alone establishes adequate consequence understanding |
| Empirical support | Conceptual cases and normal-use evidence have specific ceilings | The owner/user benefit of a hypothetical outcome is observed |
| Perspectives | Some beneficiary/source lenses were not independently sampled | Two prompts or lenses within one agent prove independent framing |

**J2 — MarginalLearningJudgment split.** N section 3 supports *retrospective local repetition* in familiar design-fiction, optionality and planner-seed examples. It conditionally forecasts that **another generic essay on the same premise** would add little, while preserving a potentially valuable different source relationship or authentic independent inquiry. A recommendation to spend attention elsewhere requires its own comparison under N's purpose. The observed counts 30, 18 and 11 are **inventories**, not a normalized creativity rate.

**Uncertainty / what would change it:** A consequential changed beneficiary, a genuinely independent pre-archive question, a different source relationship or newly available observation can justify a new bounded inquiry; no mandatory new divergent pass or staged transition is implied.

## 3. Case C2 — A navigational near duplicate preserves both expressions

**Original source expressions (not synonyms silently created here):**

- **D / DE-F2 — Design-fiction workshop:** tell a clearly hypothetical source-grounded story of a person using a future capability; D itself acknowledges GD-D overlap.

**Producing comparison:** C section 3, reaffirmed in N section 4.
**F2 — Assessment frame:** whether these source expressions make **materially different method/outcome units** for a qualitative accumulated-landscape comparison; do not ask whether the original authors made identical token choices.

**J3 — ConsolidationJudgment:** C calls them *near duplicates* because both dramatize hypothetical product experience. This allows one consolidated **method** home under the current comparison while retaining both original authored IDs, dates, source paths and different phrasings. It does **not** assert global \`sameAs\`, authorize retiring an original expression, or establish real user value.

**C2 view:** G/GD-D and D/DE-F2 belong to a common experience-rehearsal method for this purpose. A view may also group either under human experience, design fiction or creative transformations for another purpose. **Counterexample:** G/GD-G and D/DE-J3 form another near-duplicate *optionality* method, but there is no implication that the four expressions together are one product. Likewise, related architecture transformations can share an outcome while differing in operands and operations. Similarity across *different criteria* does not compose transitively.

**Unknown:** no independent beneficiary preference for one form over another and no canonical ProductPossibility identity was created by C. Grouping is not a disposition change.

A later reader should be able to answer the twelve local [PAO-CQ](possibility-assessment-competency-v1.md) questions without relying on private conversation memory. In particular:

- Can the reader identify the **source-file containing revision** (this document's pinned \`4f732...\`) separately from A/C/H/B/N/F's internally inspected revisions?
- Can they explain why A was **sufficient for convergence** without claiming all possibilities exhausted?
- Can they explain why historical repetition, forecast marginal learning and attention allocation are different judgments?
- Can they recover *both* GD-D and DE-F2, their assessed near-duplicate criterion and no \`sameAs\`?
- Can they distinguish one question using **M1** from the same question using **M2**, and both from the strategic Futures direction?
- Can they explain why Exit/Public are **retained but outside the comparison** rather than ranked last, tied or simply forgotten?
- Can they distinguish H's global directional ranking from B's pairwise preference and N's residual-question ordering?
- Can they state at least one genuine \`NOT_ASSESSED\` issue without inventing a measured user benefit or observed multi-agent result?

**Evidence ceiling:** this artifact reconstructs earlier authors' documented judgments as interpreted by this annotation. It is not a new ranking, a source-only-vs-graph comparison, an independent agent qualification, an endorsement of a product future or a construction directive. Original sources control their own meaning.
