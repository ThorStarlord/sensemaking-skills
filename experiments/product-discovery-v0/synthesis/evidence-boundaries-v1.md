# Evidence boundaries v1

## What repository reasoning can establish

- What contracts, ADRs, packages, and historical reports claim.
- Whether stated directions conflict or outrun recorded evidence.
- Whether a proposed mechanism already exists.
- Whether alternatives are logically distinct and what each assumes.
- Which evidence would discriminate alternatives.

## What it cannot establish

| Question | Required reality |
|---|---|
| Does a second attempt improve decisions? | matched blind runs plus substantive review and action before/after |
| Is active-agent execution preferable? | paired executors, actual cost/latency, maintainer ratings |
| Is approval understandable/proportionate? | observed users, boundary exercises, real consequence model |
| Do users want auto-transition? | actual accept/override/stop behavior and downstream regret |
| Is product discovery valuable? | prospective decisions and observed changes in action/outcome |

## Evidence ladder

1. Repository assertion or proposed ADR.
2. Implemented mechanism and deterministic test.
3. Executed artifact under frozen conditions.
4. Substantive independent review.
5. Matched comparative result.
6. External maintainer action/usefulness.
7. Repeated outcome across contexts.

Do not promote a lower rung into a higher-rung claim. In particular, preparation packages are not results, structural validity is not substantive correctness, and agent-generated user predictions are not user evidence.

## Human-required decisions

Humans own acceptable harm, spend/privacy tradeoffs, whether an output is actionable, and final product direction. Agents can recommend a probe but cannot authorize consequential execution on a person's behalf.
