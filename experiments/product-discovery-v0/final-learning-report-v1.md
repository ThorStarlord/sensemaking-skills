# Final learning report v1

## Executive conclusion

The lightweight discovery process improved the **quality of reasoning** in all five selected Sensemaking Skills decisions: each binary “which architecture?” question became a conditional decision with an explicit evidence gap and a cheaper next test. It did not prove that any resulting choice improves user outcomes. The most defensible conclusion is therefore: **keep the process manual, run it prospectively with real decision owners, and build no product-discovery skill yet.**

## Answers to the nine required questions

### 1. Did it improve actual decisions?

Yes, at the level available in this task. It changed:

- “multiple attempts” to a test of marginal decision-changing information;
- “which executor?” to a matched blind comparison rather than provider infrastructure;
- “how much governance?” to consequence/control mapping;
- “auto-route?” to observed accept/override behavior and reversibility;
- “build product-sensemaker?” to a manual prospective test.

This is artifact-based improvement, not external outcome validation.

### 2. Which steps repeatedly created useful learning?

Most useful were: separating observation from interpretation, generating conditional and do-nothing alternatives, identifying the uncertainty most likely to reverse the decision, marking the reality boundary, designing the cheapest credible probe, predeclaring disconfirming/ambiguous evidence, and synthesizing a confidence-bounded update.

### 3. Which steps were ceremony?

The full lifecycle taxonomy and ten repeated headings were heavier than needed. Exact uncertainty classification rarely changed the probe. Original hypothesis/update/disposition can be combined. Kill criteria are useful for costly repeated probes but formulaic for a one-time document review. A six-block worksheet is enough.

### 4. What evidence could not be generated by an agent?

User demand; maintainer actionability; accept/override behavior; approval comprehension; risk tolerance; matched executor quality; real cost/latency; whether a second attempt changes action; and later outcome value. These are marked `REQUIRES_REAL_WORLD_EVIDENCE` in the cases rather than invented.

### 5. Is a `product-sensemaker` justified?

No. One concierge analysis demonstrates a useful questioning pattern, not demand for a broad capability. A broad skill risks synthetic user evidence, universal routing, and premature infrastructure.

### 6. Are narrower skills justified?

Not yet. `decision-probe-design`, `evidence-synthesis`, and `evidence-boundary-check` repeated across cases, but a short prompt/checklist currently performs the mechanics. Their status is `KEEP_MANUAL`; prospective repeated failure or inconsistency would be needed before a narrow prototype.

### 7. What should remain manual?

Decision ownership, consequence/value judgments, selection of acceptable evidence, human substantive review, consent, maintainer usefulness assessment, and interpretation of ambiguous results. Agents can prepare alternatives, evidence maps, and probe contracts.

### 8. What is the cheapest next experiment?

Apply the six-block worksheet to five prospective owner decisions. Capture intended action before/after, evidence sought, time burden, and later update. Include at least one do-nothing outcome. Do not add infrastructure. The owner—not the agent—rates usefulness.

### 9. What should not be built yet?

Do not build a product-sensemaker, discovery router/lifecycle, provider selector, multi-attempt orchestrator, auto-transition mechanism, discovery schema/validator, campaign runner, or additional governance machinery. Do not rewrite the North Star.

## Method verdict

The lifecycle hypothesis was only partly supported. The useful causal sequence was:

`decision → observed evidence/unknowns → genuine alternatives → decision-changing uncertainty → cheapest credible probe → reality → update`

“Construction” and “assurance” are not universal stages; they are work types selected when evidence and consequence justify them. Problem/solution discovery and validation labels help orientation but should not become gates.

## Evidence basis and limitations

Evidence used includes the repo-sensemaker boundary, agent-native ADR, Stage 2 recommendation/selection split, EXP-0002 preparation package and scientific questions, ADR 0023 authorization design, and July product-contract review. The review reports zero successful end-to-end external golden paths and no provider/platform comparison (`docs/PRODUCT-CONTRACT-REVIEW-2026-07-26.md:18-19,303`). No current hardening-v1 artifacts were available in this checkout, and none were inferred or modified. Repository reports are themselves project evidence, not independently verified external facts.

## Final recommendation

**KEEP_MANUAL.** Run the prospective five-decision concierge test. Reconsider only a narrow `decision-probe-design` or `evidence-boundary-check` skill if repeated sessions show a stable input/output, material owner value, and a recurring failure that a prompt alone does not solve.
