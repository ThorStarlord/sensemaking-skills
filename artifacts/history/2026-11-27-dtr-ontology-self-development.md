## 1. Ontological divergence and reconciliation

Experience-first: an agent must compare future capability states, understand
what creates or destroys options, relate paths across genuine changes and
recognize which identities and premises are inherited. Architecture-first:
the existing prospective, possibility, connected inquiry and evolution/learning
ontologies already own states, paths, transitions, conditional effects and
reconsideration. The missing conceptual unity is how conditions connect and
change trajectories, not another name for every existing entity.

Alternatives: retain guidance alone (low cost, weak explicit semantic
distinctions); add a comprehensive typed developmental graph (expressive, but
duplicates current identity and persistence ownership without a demonstrated
consumer); add a conceptual specialization with specific endpoint and epistemic
rules and selectively improve a real existing consumer (selected). Existing
source-grounded records and artifacts remain the owners. The explicit
[ontology](../../docs/semantic-architecture/developmental-trajectory-reasoning-ontology.md)
distinguishes role, relation, persistence and machine representation. In
particular shared enabler, optionality, path dependence and recombination are
not just new nouns; each has a precise relationship/epistemic obligation.
Formal additional kinds/predicates remain an open, evidence-driven choice.
